\begin{abstract}
Hamilton's analogy places classical mechanics beside geometrical optics and wave mechanics beside wave optics, and Schr\"odinger's equation was obtained by taking the step from the first pair to the second.
The 0-Sphere model has so far stood on the first rung: internal energy transport follows thermal geodesics of an optical metric, and the accumulated internal phase is a Hamilton principal function of the arc between the two kernels.
This paper takes the second step.
Writing the internal wave as $A\,e^{i\mathcal{S}/\hbar}$ separates, order by order in $\hbar$, a Hamilton--Jacobi equation for the phase from a continuity equation for $A^2$ transported at the group velocity, and thereby separates two refractive indices that the series had identified: a phase index governing stationary phase and a group index governing energy transport.
The group index $\ngr = 1/\bZB = 24.71$ does not depend on the origin of energy; the phase index of a matter wave does, taking the value $\bZB$ when the rest energy is kept and $2/\bZB$ when it is removed.
The closure of the internal cycle on a round trip of length $\lambdaC$ fixes the ratio of the two indices of the internal carrier, not its dispersion. A body of mass $m_e$ making the round trip at $v_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ holds $0.0405$ of its de~Broglie wavelength and does not close, so that the internal carrier is not the de~Broglie wave of the electron's mass. If the carrier is taken non-dispersive in its lowest mode and its phase is counted with the internal phase $\theta$, the dynamical phase of the series accumulates $2\pi$ per round trip and the action is $J = h$; counted with the half angle $\theta/2$ of the amplitude, which carries the double cover of the spinor, the same round trip gives $J = h/2$.
Factoring out the rest-energy frequency $\nu_C = m_ec^2/h$, which de~Broglie attached to the electron as an internal periodic phenomenon, leaves an envelope that obeys the Schr\"odinger equation, a heat equation with imaginary diffusivity $\hbar/2m_e = (\lambdaC/2)(c/2\pi)$.
The result is a two-tier structure: an external envelope with a continuous spectrum, whose spreading is real, and an internal transfer with a discrete spectrum fixed by the energy identity, which a free Schr\"odinger packet of Compton size could not sustain, since such a packet doubles its width within $0.44$ of one internal cycle.
Because the internal phase advances uniformly, the quartic energy contains only the harmonics $0$, $\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ and $2\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ ($41.36$~keV); written as the density of a product of two amplitudes, it shows that unequal rates of the two factors would split the first line and add sum and beat lines. The internal transfer may not oscillate the charge dipole, and an electron of kinetic energy $419$~eV carries the momentum of a $20.7$~keV photon.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The wave nature of matter remained hidden for a long time because the de~Broglie wavelength is short compared with the lengths used in macroscopic observation. Yukawa states this point in his introduction to quantum mechanics~\cite{Yukawa1954} and compares it with optics: as long as the refractive index of a medium can be regarded as uniform over a distance of the order of one wavelength, the wave nature of light need not be considered, and geometrical optics applied to rays suffices. He then follows Hamilton~\cite{Hamilton1834}. The Hamilton--Jacobi equation of a particle has the same form as the eikonal equation of a ray; Maupertuis' principle of least action has the same form as Fermat's principle of least time; and Schr\"odinger~\cite{Schrodinger1926} obtained wave mechanics by passing from the ray description to a wave equation, as wave optics stands to geometrical optics.

The 0-Sphere model has developed the first half of this analogy and not the second. Paper~\#24~\cite{H24} introduced thermal geodesics, the paths of internal energy transport between the two kernels of the electron. Paper~\#51~\cite{H51} showed that the thermal metric is the optical metric $\mathcal{G}_{\mu\nu} = n^2(T)g_{\mu\nu}$ of a medium with temperature-dependent index, and its version~3 identified the dynamical phase accumulated along the arc between the kernels, $\Sdyn = \omZB\int d\tau$, as a Hamilton principal function from which a world function and the spacetime metric are obtained. Paper~\#66~\cite{H66} assigned the internal region the index $n = 1/\bZB$, so that the internal transport speed $v_{\mathrm{ZB}} = \bZB c$ is the group velocity of a carrier in a medium. These are statements about rays. No wave has been superposed, and the Schr\"odinger equation has not appeared in the series.

This paper takes the step from rays to waves. Section~\ref{sec:rays} restates the ray layer of the series in Hamilton's language. Section~\ref{sec:waves} writes the internal wave in the eikonal form and shows that it separates a phase, governed by a Hamilton--Jacobi equation, from an intensity transported at the group velocity, so that two refractive indices appear where the series had one. Section~\ref{sec:indices} evaluates the two indices and states what the closure of the internal cycle requires of the internal carrier. Section~\ref{sec:envelope} factors out the rest-energy frequency and obtains the Schr\"odinger equation for the envelope. Section~\ref{sec:twotier} sets out the resulting two-tier structure and the reason why the internal tier cannot be described by Schr\"odinger dynamics. Section~\ref{sec:discrete} places the discrete group velocity of Paper~\#66 in the line of Bohr's correspondence principle and Heisenberg's multiplication rule. Section~\ref{sec:consequences} collects the consequences that can be checked against measurement.

\paragraph{Scope.} The paper takes three inputs from the series: the canonical value $\bZB^2 = 0.00163798087$, the phase functional of Paper~\#51 in its version~3, and the internal index and discrete spectrum of Paper~\#66. It does not derive the index from the internal temperature profile, does not treat the coupling of the internal transfer to external photons beyond the constraints of Section~\ref{sec:consequences}, and does not address gravitation. The presentation of Hamilton's analogy, of the eikonal expansion and of the correspondence principle follows Ref.~\cite{Yukawa1954}, from which equation numbers are cited where a specific step is used.

\section{The Ray Layer of the Series}
\label{sec:rays}

\paragraph{Hamilton's analogy.} For a particle of energy $E$ in a potential $V$, the solution of the Hamilton--Jacobi equation can be written $W = -Et + S(\bm{x})$ with $|\nabla S| = \sqrt{2m(E - V)} = p$~\cite{Yukawa1954}. Surfaces of constant $W$ move along $\nabla S$ with the speed
\begin{equation}
u \;=\; \frac{E}{p},
\label{eq:u_HJ}
\end{equation}
and the trajectories are the orthogonal trajectories of these surfaces. Fermat's principle with the speed $u$ of the surfaces, $\delta\int ds/u = 0$, becomes Maupertuis' principle $\delta\int p\,ds = 0$ on substitution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:u_HJ}. The ray index of the mechanical problem is therefore proportional to the momentum, $n \propto p$, and $u$ is the speed of a phase surface, not of the particle.

\paragraph{The thermal geodesic.} Paper~\#51 records a hierarchy of geodesic principles~\cite{H51}. The thermal geodesic obeys $\delta\int\kappa\,ds = 0$ with $\kappa \propto n(T)$, the path of least thermal resistance along which internal energy is transported between the kernels. The phase functional obeys a separate principle: the phase accumulated along the arc is extremal. In the language of the series the two rows were stated with one index $n(T)$.

\paragraph{The dynamical phase as a principal function.} Version~3 of Paper~\#51 decomposes the phase accumulated between kernel events into a dynamical part and a geometric (Berry) part~\cite{H51, Berry1984},
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S} \;=\; \Sdyn + \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{geo}},
\qquad
\Sdyn \;=\; \omZB\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ext}}} d\tau,
\label{eq:Sdecomp}
\end{equation}
with $\omZB = 2\pi\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}} = \bZB\,m_ec^2/\hbar$. The dynamical part is symmetric under exchange of the endpoints and accumulates over a round trip, as the action variable $\oint p\,dq$ does~\cite{Yukawa1954}; the geometric part is antisymmetric and carries spin and chirality. The dynamical part is a Hamilton principal function of the arc, a generating function of the first kind in the endpoints, and its square defines the world function $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}} = \Sdyn^2/(2\omZB^2)$ from which the metric is reconstructed~\cite{H51, Synge1960}. All of this belongs to the ray layer: it concerns the extremal arc and the phase accumulated along it, not a wave superposed from several arcs or several frequencies.

The step from rays to waves is taken by writing a wave whose phase is the principal function. For a system with Hamiltonian $H(q,p)$, the substitution
\begin{equation}
\psi(q,t) \;=\; A(q,t)\,e^{iS(q,t)/\hbar},
\label{eq:eikonal}
\end{equation}
with real $A$ and $S$, into the wave equation obtained by $p \to -i\hbar\,\partial/\partial q$, and the separation of real and imaginary parts at the two lowest orders in $\hbar$, give two equations~\cite{Yukawa1954}. At order $\hbar^0$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial S}{\partial t} + H\!\left(q, \frac{\partial S}{\partial q}\right) \;=\; 0,
\label{eq:HJ}
\end{equation}
the Hamilton--Jacobi equation, so that the phase of the wave is the classical action. At order $\hbar^1$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial A^2}{\partial t} + \sum_r \frac{\partial}{\partial q_r}\!\left(A^2\,\frac{dq_r}{dt}\right) \;=\; 0,
\qquad
\frac{dq_r}{dt} \;=\; \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_r},
\label{eq:continuity}
\end{equation}
a continuity equation in which $A^2$ is carried with the velocity $\partial H/\partial p$. Yukawa describes Eq.~\eqref{eq:continuity} as the continuity equation of a fluid~\cite{Yukawa1954}; Madelung~\cite{Madelung1927} had written the same pair as the equations of a fluid whose density is $A^2$, with a correction of order $\hbar^2$ that the ray limit drops.

The velocity $\partial H/\partial p = d\omega/dk$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:continuity} is the group velocity. The two orders therefore carry two different speeds. The phase $S$ advances with the phase velocity of Eq.~\eqref{eq:u_HJ}, and its rays obey Fermat's principle with the phase index. The intensity $A^2$, which carries the energy, advances with the group velocity, and its transport time is set by the group index. The two rows of the geodesic hierarchy of Paper~\#51 are the two orders of the eikonal expansion:
\begin{align}
\text{order } \hbar^0:&\quad \text{phase } S,\ \text{Hamilton--Jacobi},\ \text{phase index},
\notag\\
\text{order } \hbar^1:&\quad \text{intensity } A^2,\ \text{continuity},\ \text{group index}.
\label{eq:twoorders}
\end{align}
The phase functional of Paper~\#51 belongs to the first line; the thermal geodesic, a path of energy transport, belongs to the second. Table~\ref{tab:yukawa} lists the correspondences used in this paper.

The 0-Sphere model already distinguishes three levels of the internal state~\cite{H65, H66}: an amplitude $\cos(\iphase/2)$, the share of the energy held at a kernel $\cos^2(\iphase/2)$, and the energy held at a kernel $\cos^4(\iphase/2)$, the last being the square of the share. In the eikonal form, the amplitude is $A$, linear and subject to superposition; the share is $A^2$, the quantity transported by Eq.~\eqref{eq:continuity} and the counterpart of Born's probability density; and the energy is the quartic level on which the discrete spectrum of Paper~\#66 is built. Linear superposition is a property of the amplitude level only.

\paragraph{Two indices of a matter wave.} De~Broglie obtained the matter wave by applying a Lorentz transformation to the rest-energy oscillation $m_ec^2 = h\nu_0$~\cite{deBroglie1925, Yukawa1954}. In a frame in which the electron moves at $v$, the wave has frequency $E/h$ and wavelength $h/p$, its phase surfaces move at $u = E/p = c^2/v$, and a group formed from neighbouring wavelengths moves at $d\nu/d(1/\lambda) = dE/dp = v$. With the rest energy kept, the phase and group indices are
\begin{equation}
\nph \;=\; \frac{c}{u} \;=\; \frac{v}{c},
\qquad
\ngr \;=\; \frac{c}{v},
\qquad
\nph\,\ngr \;=\; 1.
\label{eq:indices_rel}
\end{equation}
When the rest energy is removed, as in the non-relativistic wave equation, $E = p^2/2m$ and the phase velocity becomes $u = p/2m = v/2$~\cite{Yukawa1954}, so that $\nph = 2c/v$. The group velocity $dE/dp = v$ is the same in both cases.

\paragraph{The phase index depends on the origin of energy.} Shifting the energy by a constant multiplies every component of a wave by the same phase factor $e^{-i\,\mathrm{const}\,t/\hbar}$. It changes the phase velocity of every component and leaves the group velocity unchanged, because the group velocity is a derivative of the energy. For a particle moving at $v_{\mathrm{ZB}} = \bZB c$ the values are collected in Table~\ref{tab:indices}. Paper~\#66 assigned the internal region the index $1/\bZB = 24.71$, identifying its phase and group indices~\cite{H66}. Of the two, the group index is the quantity that does not depend on how the energy is counted, and it is the one that sets the round-trip time and the internal frequency. Any statement that uses the phase index, such as Snell's law and the critical angle of Paper~\#66, requires a statement of the origin from which the internal phase is counted.


\paragraph{Closure of the internal cycle.} The internal transfer of the 0-Sphere model is periodic: one cycle of the internal phase $\iphase$ is one round trip of the photon sphere between the kernels, a path of length $\lambdaC$, and the internal frequency is defined by $\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}} = v_{\mathrm{ZB}}/\lambdaC$~\cite{H51, H66}. A periodic wave on a closed path must hold an integer number of wavelengths, the condition $2\pi a = n\lambda$ by which de~Broglie recovered Bohr's quantization $\oint p\,dq = nh$~\cite{deBroglie1925, Yukawa1954}. A wave of frequency $\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ and group velocity $v_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ that holds $N$ wavelengths on the round trip has phase velocity $v_{\mathrm{ZB}}/N$, so that the closure fixes the ratio $\nph/\ngr = N$ of its two indices and leaves its dispersion free. The lowest mode, $N = 1$, is the non-dispersive carrier, with wavelength
\begin{equation}
\lambda \;=\; \frac{v_{\mathrm{ZB}}}{\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}} \;=\; \lambdaC,
\label{eq:lambda_carrier}
\end{equation}
which restates the definition of $\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}$; the mode $N = 2$ has $\nph = 2/\bZB$, the value in the second row of Table~\ref{tab:indices}. A body of mass $m_e$ making the round trip at $v_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ has the de~Broglie wavelength $h/(\gamma m_ev_{\mathrm{ZB}}) = 59.90$~pm, and the round trip holds $\gamma\bZB = 0.0405$ of it; such a body does not satisfy the closure, and the internal carrier is not the de~Broglie wave of the electron's mass moving at the internal speed. The closure does not exclude a dispersive carrier of a different mass: a carrier of mass $m_e/(\gamma\bZB) = 24.7\,m_e$ moving at $v_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ has wavelength $\lambdaC$. The de~Broglie wave of the electron's mass, with $\nph = \bZB$ and $\ngr = 1/\bZB$, belongs to the motion of the electron as a whole, which is the subject of Section~\ref{sec:envelope}.

\paragraph{The dynamical phase and the action.} Take the carrier to be the non-dispersive lowest mode and identify its phase with $\iphase$. The dynamical phase of Paper~\#51 advances at $\omZB = \bZB\,m_ec^2/\hbar$, and over one internal period $1/\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ it accumulates
\begin{align}
\Delta\Sdyn &\;=\; \frac{\omZB}{\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}} \;=\; 2\pi,
\notag\\
J &\;=\; \oint p\,dq \;=\; \hbar\,\Delta\Sdyn \;=\; h,
\label{eq:Jh}
\end{align}
with the canonical momentum $h/\lambdaC = m_ec$ of the carrier. The two lines are consistent readings of the stated assumptions, not derivations. Identifying the carrier phase with $\iphase$ makes $\Delta\Sdyn = 2\pi$ by the definition of the cycle; and over one period the ratio of the time phase $E/(\hbar\nu)$ to the space phase $J/\hbar$ is $\ngr/\nph$, so that $J = \hbar\,\Delta\Sdyn$ is the non-dispersive condition $\nph = \ngr$. The value $2\pi$ is exact when $\tau$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sdecomp} is the proper time of the rest frame of the electron; counted along a worldline moving at $v_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ it is $2\pi/\gamma$, smaller by $8.2\times10^{-4}$. In the same terms, the factor $\bZB$ in $\omZB$ is the ratio $\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}/\nu_C$ of the frequency that the carrier has at the group velocity to the frequency it would have at the invariant speed, as stated in Paper~\#66~\cite{H66}; this restates that identification and does not derive $\bZB$, which Paper~\#51 leaves open~\cite{H51}. The closure is imposed here on the cycle of $\iphase$, whose frequency is $\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}$. It is not imposed on the line at $2\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}$, and the condition $n(2\omega) = n(\omega)$ of Paper~\#66 remains open as stated there.

\paragraph{The wavelength equals the size of the region.} Equation~\eqref{eq:lambda_carrier} also places the internal region with respect to Yukawa's criterion for geometrical optics. The ray limit requires $|\partial S/\partial q|\,A \gg \hbar\,|\partial A/\partial q|$~\cite{Yukawa1954}, that is, a phase that varies much faster than the amplitude. Over a region of size $L = \lambdaC$ the ratio of the two sides is of order $2\pi L/\lambda = 2\pi$ for the internal carrier. The interior of the electron is therefore at the border between the ray regime and the wave regime: the index varies over a distance of one wavelength, which is the situation in which, by Yukawa's criterion, the wave nature cannot be neglected. Statements of Paper~\#66 that rest on geometrical optics, Snell's law and its critical angle in particular, are to be read with this qualification, and the critical angle depends in addition on the phase index, and so on the origin from which the internal phase is counted (Table~\ref{tab:indices}); the interior is better described as a resonator in its fundamental mode, with one wavelength on the round trip.

\section{Carrier and Envelope: the Schr\"odinger Equation}
\label{sec:envelope}

\paragraph{The subtraction of the rest energy.} Before writing the wave equation of a particle in a potential, Yukawa removes the rest energy $m_ec^2$ and uses $E = T + V = h\nu$ with the non-relativistic energy~\cite{Yukawa1954}; in the Pauli limit of the Dirac equation the same step reappears as $E = W - m_ec^2$. The step is taken in one line in each place, and the oscillation $\nu_0 = m_ec^2/h$ that de~Broglie attached to the electron a few pages earlier does not reappear. In the 0-Sphere model this oscillation is the light-speed reference rate $\nu_C$ at which the phase of each Dirac branch advances~\cite{H51, H65}.

\paragraph{The envelope equation.} The subtraction has a direct meaning in wave terms. The 0-Sphere model retains Lorentz covariance and the invariant speed $c$ without modification~\cite{H51, H66}, and assigns to the electron at rest the energy $m_ec^2$. For a system whose energy and momentum form a four-vector and whose energy at rest is $m_ec^2$, the only covariant relation between them is $E^2 = p^2c^2 + m_e^2c^4$. A wave of the electron as a whole, with $E = \hbar\omega$ and $\bm{p} = \hbar\bm{k}$, therefore satisfies the Klein--Gordon form $(\partial_t^2/c^2 - \nabla^2 + m_e^2c^2/\hbar^2)\Psi = 0$, which each component of the free Dirac spinor also obeys. The passage from the relation between $E$ and $p$ to this wave equation uses the Planck--de~Broglie relations and a linear field of a single component; these are the standard quantum inputs and are taken here as given. That the energy and momentum of an extended system with internal flow form a four-vector requires in addition that its internal stresses integrate to zero in the rest frame, the condition of von~Laue, which connects to the recoil closure of the internal dynamics (Section~\ref{sec:consequences}). Writing
\begin{equation}
\Psi(\bm{x},t) \;=\; e^{-2\pi i\nu_C t}\,\varphi(\bm{x},t),
\label{eq:carrier}
\end{equation}
and assuming that $\varphi$ varies slowly on the time scale $1/\nu_C$, so that $|\partial_t^2\varphi| \ll 4\pi\nu_C|\partial_t\varphi|$, and keeping the positive-frequency branch, gives
\begin{equation}
i\hbar\,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t} \;=\; -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_e}\nabla^2\varphi.
\label{eq:schrodinger}
\end{equation}
The Schr\"odinger equation is the equation of the envelope of a carrier at the rest-energy frequency. In optics the same operation, factoring out a fast carrier and neglecting the second derivative of the envelope, is the slowly varying envelope approximation, and it turns the Helmholtz equation into the paraxial wave equation, which has the form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:schrodinger}~\cite{Siegman1986}.

\paragraph{The imaginary heat equation.} Equation~\eqref{eq:schrodinger} has the form of a heat equation, $\partial_t\varphi = \kappa_{\mathrm{Q}}\nabla^2\varphi$, with the purely imaginary diffusivity $\kappa_{\mathrm{Q}} = i\hbar/2m_e$, as Yukawa notes~\cite{Yukawa1954}. Its magnitude is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\hbar}{2m_e} \;=\; \frac{\lambdaC}{2}\cdot\frac{c}{2\pi} \;=\; 5.79\times10^{-5}\ \text{m}^2/\text{s},
\label{eq:diffusivity}
\end{equation}
half the round trip, which is the separation of the kernels, times the invariant speed divided by $2\pi$. The 0-Sphere model has described the internal transport in thermal language since Paper~\#24, and Paper~\#51 noted that its thermal metric is in fact a phase metric~\cite{H51}. The relation between a heat equation and the Schr\"odinger equation is the substitution of an imaginary for a real diffusivity; the thermal language of the series and the phase language of wave mechanics are related by that substitution. The symbol $\kappa_{\mathrm{Q}}$ is used here to distinguish this diffusivity from the thermal resistance $\kappa$ of Paper~\#51.

\section{Two Tiers: External Envelope and Internal Transfer}
\label{sec:twotier}

Figure~\ref{fig:twotier} summarizes the structure that results. The carrier at $\nu_C$ is common to both tiers. The external tier is the envelope $\varphi$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:schrodinger}: it describes the motion of the electron as a whole, it is built from a continuous spectrum of plane waves, a Gaussian window is an admissible initial condition, and its spreading is real and observed. The internal tier is the transfer between the kernels described by the energy identity of the series: its spectrum consists of the three lines $0$, $\omega$ and $2\omega$ with weights fixed by the identity~\cite{H66}, and its envelope is restored at every cycle.

\paragraph{A Schr\"odinger packet of Compton size.} The internal tier cannot be described by Eq.~\eqref{eq:schrodinger}. A free packet $\varphi \propto e^{-x^2/2a^2}$ of initial width $a$ grows as $a\sqrt{1 + (\hbar t/m_ea^2)^2}$ and doubles its width after $T = \sqrt{3}\,m_ea^2/\hbar$~\cite{Yukawa1954}. Yukawa's example, $a = 10^{-8}$~cm, gives $T \approx 10^{-16}$~s. For a packet confined to the internal scale, $a = \lambdaC$,
\begin{align}
T &\;=\; 2\pi\sqrt{3}\,\frac{\lambdaC}{c} \;=\; 8.81\times10^{-20}\ \text{s},
\notag\\
T\,\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}} &\;=\; 2\pi\sqrt{3}\,\bZB \;=\; 0.44.
\label{eq:doubling}
\end{align}
In general $T\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}} = 0.44\,(a/\lambdaC)^2$, which stays below one half for $a < 1.07\,\lambdaC$ and below one cycle for $a < 1.51\,\lambdaC$; the internal region, of size $\lambdaC/2$, lies well inside both bounds. A free Schr\"odinger packet of the size of the internal structure doubles its width in less than half of one internal cycle. The recurrence of the same internal envelope at every cycle, stated in Paper~\#66, is therefore not a property of the free envelope equation. A binding potential could supply recurrence, as for the coherent states of the harmonic oscillator, but the model provides no such potential for the internal transfer; the recurrence is supplied by the energy identity, which holds at every phase. The statement of Paper~\#66 that this recurrence keeps the electron localized applies to the internal tier only; the external envelope spreads.

\paragraph{The Gordon decomposition.} The standard theory contains a decomposition of the same shape. The probability current of the Dirac equation is $c\,\bar{\psi}\bm{\alpha}\psi$, in which the velocity operator $c\bm{\alpha}$ with eigenvalues $\pm c$ appears, while the current of the Schr\"odinger equation is the convective current of the group velocity $\bm{p}/m_e$. Gordon~\cite{Gordon1928} showed that the Dirac current is the sum of a convective current of Schr\"odinger form and a spin current of the form $\nabla\times(\bar{\psi}\bm{\sigma}\psi)$, together with a polarization term that is a time derivative. The spin current has zero divergence and transports no net charge; it circulates within the region occupied by the electron. The two tiers of Fig.~\ref{fig:twotier} correspond to the two terms: the external envelope to the convective current, and the internal transfer to the divergence-free circulation, to which the trembling motion at $\pm c$ belongs.

\section{Discrete Group Velocity and the Correspondence Principle}
\label{sec:discrete}

\paragraph{From derivative to difference quotient.} Paper~\#66 obtained the group velocity of the internal carrier as a difference quotient between two discrete lines, $v_g = \Delta\omega/\Delta k$, and the group index $\ngr = 2n(2\omega) - n(\omega)$~\cite{H66}. The replacement of a derivative by a difference quotient over discrete values has a long precedent. In the classical theory of multiply periodic motion, the frequency of the motion is $\nu = \partial E/\partial J$ and its harmonics are $\tau\,\partial E/\partial J$ with integer $\tau$~\cite{Yukawa1954}. Bohr's correspondence principle replaces this derivative by the difference quotient $\nu = [E(n) - E(n - \tau)]/h$ over the quantum numbers, with $\Delta J = h$, and identifies the $\tau$-th classical harmonic with the transition $\Delta n = \tau$. In the treatment of the Zeeman effect the angular group velocity of the rotating wave is written $d\omega/dm$ over the integer magnetic quantum number~\cite{Yukawa1954}. The group velocity of Paper~\#66 is a further instance of the same replacement. Table~\ref{tab:discrete} places the three side by side.

\paragraph{Heisenberg's multiplication rule and the second line.} Paper~\#66 obtains the line at $2\omega$ by squaring the share $\cos^2(\iphase/2) = \tfrac12(1 + \cos\iphase)$, a classical product of Fourier series in which frequencies add. Heisenberg's starting point in 1925 was that this rule is incompatible with the Ritz combination principle: the frequencies of quantum transitions combine as $\nu(n, n-\alpha) + \nu(n-\alpha, n-\alpha-\beta) = \nu(n, n-\alpha-\beta)$, and the classical product of two Fourier series produces sums that do not respect it, so that the product had to be replaced by the product of matrices~\cite{Heisenberg1925, Yukawa1954}. Translated into this language, the second line of the internal spectrum corresponds to a two-step combination $\nu(n, n-1) + \nu(n-1, n-2)$, and it equals $2\nu(n, n-1)$ only when the three levels involved are equally spaced. The harmonic oscillator is the case in which classical and quantum frequencies agree for this reason~\cite{Yukawa1954}. In the 0-Sphere model the internal phase advances uniformly, $\iphase = \omega t$, and every function of a uniformly advancing phase contains only integer harmonics. The squaring of the share gives the second line at exactly twice the first,
\begin{align}
\cos^4\!\frac{\iphase}{2} &\;=\; \frac38 + \frac12\cos\iphase + \frac18\cos2\iphase,
\notag\\
\nu_2 &\;=\; 2\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}} \;=\; 1.0001\times10^{19}\ \text{Hz}\ \ (41.36\ \text{keV}).
\label{eq:second_line}
\end{align}
The agreement of the classical squaring with the matrix rule is therefore the agreement that holds for equally spaced levels, as for the harmonic oscillator, and it is not a separate result. The value $2\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ is the frequency of the second Fourier component of the energy held at a kernel; that this component is emitted as radiation is not shown here.

\paragraph{The energy as a product density.} The same spectrum can be written in the language of the matrix rule. The amplitude $\cos(\iphase/2) = \tfrac12(e^{i\iphase/2} + e^{-i\iphase/2})$ has two components, at $\pm\omega/2$. The share, bilinear in the amplitude, contains the differences $0$ and $\pm\omega$. The energy is the square of the share; written as a bilinear quantity, it is the density of the product $a\otimes a$ of two copies of the amplitude, whose components lie at the sums of the frequencies of the factors,
\begin{equation}
\left\{\pm\tfrac{\omega}{2}\right\} + \left\{\pm\tfrac{\omega}{2}\right\} \;=\; \{+\omega,\ 0,\ 0,\ -\omega\}.
\label{eq:triplet}
\end{equation}
These levels are equally spaced, as the sums of any two-element set with itself are, and their differences $0$, $\pm\omega$ and $\pm2\omega$ reproduce Eq.~\eqref{eq:second_line}. For identical factors the product form is a rewriting of the double-angle identity and adds no constraint. Its use is that it states what changes when the factors are not identical. If the factors advance with phases $\iphase_A = \omega_At$ and $\iphase_B = \omega_Bt$,
\begin{align}
\cos^2\!\frac{\iphase_A}{2}\,\cos^2\!\frac{\iphase_B}{2}
&\;=\; \frac14 + \frac14\cos\iphase_A + \frac14\cos\iphase_B
\notag\\
&\quad + \frac18\cos(\iphase_A + \iphase_B) + \frac18\cos(\iphase_A - \iphase_B):
\label{eq:detuned}
\end{align}
the line at $\omega$ splits into lines at $\omega_A$ and $\omega_B$, the line at $2\omega$ moves to $\omega_A + \omega_B$, and a line appears at the beat $|\omega_A - \omega_B|$, far below the internal frequency for a small detuning. The identification of the two factors with the two kernels, by which a detuning $\omega_A \neq \omega_B$ of the kernels would appear in this form, is not established here; for the electron, with identical factors, the spectrum is that of Paper~\#66.

\paragraph{The layer of the Dirac frequency.} The same language restates the factor of two between the Dirac and 0-Sphere frequencies that Paper~\#66 left open~\cite{H66}. In a linear theory an observable density is bilinear in the amplitude, and the frequencies it contains are differences of the frequencies of the amplitude. The Dirac Zitterbewegung frequency $2m_ec^2/h$ is of this kind: the difference between the branches at $\pm m_ec^2$ appears in the bilinear layer. In the 0-Sphere model the internal phase appears at the frequency $\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ in the share, which is bilinear in the amplitude, and at $2\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ in the energy, which is bilinear in the product~\eqref{eq:triplet}. Whether the Dirac frequency is to be compared with the line of the share or with that of the energy is a question about which layer is observed, and whether the ratio $\nu_{\mathrm{D}}/\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}} = 2/\bZB$ contains a factor of two beyond $1/\bZB$ depends on its answer. This restates the open item of Paper~\#66 without closing it.

\section{Consequences and Constraints}
\label{sec:consequences}

\paragraph{The momentum of the internal line.} A photon of energy $\bZB m_ec^2$ carries the momentum $\bZB m_ec$, which is the momentum of an electron moving at $v_{\mathrm{ZB}}$. The vacuum wavelength of the internal line is $c/\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}} = 59.95$~pm, and the de~Broglie wavelength of an electron with kinetic energy $(\gamma - 1)m_ec^2 = 419.0$~eV, the energy of the $\beta^2$ layer of the series, is $59.90$~pm; the difference, $8\times10^{-4}$, is the Lorentz factor. The identity holds for any $\bZB$ and does not select its value. Its content is that one quantum of the internal line carries the vacuum momentum $\bZB m_ec$ of a body of mass $m_e$ moving at the internal speed. The canonical momentum of the non-dispersive carrier of Section~\ref{sec:indices} in the internal medium is $h/\lambdaC = m_ec$, larger by the index $1/\bZB$; the two are the vacuum and medium (Minkowski) momenta of the same quantum, and which of them enters the momentum balance of a carrier that reverses its direction at a kernel is the question of the recoil closure of the internal dynamics, which is not developed here.

\paragraph{The internal transfer does not oscillate the charge dipole.} If the charge of the electron moved between the kernels at the internal frequency, it would radiate as an oscillating dipole. In the quantum expression for the total emitted power, $(4/3)(e^2\omega^4/c^3)|r|^2$~\cite{Yukawa1954}, $|r|$ is a matrix element, which corresponds to half the amplitude $x_0$ of a classical oscillation. At $\omega = 2\pi\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ an amplitude $x_0 = \lambdaC/4$, half the separation of the kernels, gives $1.0$~W, which would exhaust the rest energy in $8.0\times10^{-14}$~s; an amplitude $x_0 = \lambdaC/2$ gives $4.1$~W and $2.0\times10^{-14}$~s. The free electron is stable, so the internal transfer cannot carry an oscillating charge dipole. The transfer admitted by the model is one that leaves the centre of charge fixed, which is the form of the divergence-free spin current of the Gordon decomposition (Section~\ref{sec:twotier}). This sharpens the statement of Paper~\#66 that the internal transfer does not radiate~\cite{H66}.

\paragraph{Scattering.} A naive reading of the internal index as a polarizable sphere would give Rayleigh scattering, with a cross section that vanishes as $\omega^4$ below a resonance. A free electron instead scatters low-frequency light with the frequency-independent Thomson cross section, which is verified routinely in laser scattering from plasmas. Had the whole electron responded as an oscillator with resonance at $20.7$~keV, scattering at $2.33$~eV would be suppressed by $(2.33\ \text{eV}/20.7\ \text{keV})^4 = 1.6\times10^{-16}$. The two-tier structure removes this conflict: the centre of mass, a free charge, gives the Thomson and Klein--Nishina cross sections, and the internal tier can contribute at most a resonant feature near $20.7$~keV in the electron rest frame. Compton scattering at $17$--$22$~keV and with synchrotron radiation across this band has been measured extensively and agrees with the Klein--Nishina formula; these data bound the strength and width of any such feature. Extracting that bound is left as a task; it is the same measurement as the X-ray search proposed in Paper~\#66.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{The count of the internal phase.} Section~\ref{sec:indices} takes the internal carrier to be non-dispersive in its lowest mode and counts its phase with $\iphase$. Under these assumptions $\Delta\Sdyn = 2\pi$ and $J = h$ are consistent, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Jh}; without them the closure fixes only the ratio $\nph/\ngr = N$. The count with $\iphase$ is itself a reading. The amplitude of the model is $\cos(\iphase/2)$, whose half angle expresses the double cover of the spinor and returns to its initial value only after $4\pi$~\cite{H65, H29}. Counted with $\iphase/2$, the same round trip accumulates $\pi$ and gives $J = h/2$, the form of the unit of action of spin $\tfrac12$. For a libration between two turning points the lowest Bohr--Sommerfeld value is set by the Maslov index, and both $h$ and $h/2$ occur. Nothing in the present paper decides between the two counts. A description of the internal transfer by the energy identity alone does not require a carrier wave.

\paragraph{The rate of the dynamical phase.} The reading of the factor $\bZB$ in $\omZB = \bZB\,m_ec^2/\hbar$ as the ratio $\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}/\nu_C$ restates the identification of Paper~\#66. It does not derive $\bZB$ from the propagation of the internal carrier, which is the open item of Paper~\#51, and $\bZB$ remains an input from the anomalous magnetic moment.

\paragraph{The rate of a Dirac branch.} Section~\ref{sec:envelope} and Table~\ref{tab:yukawa} take the rest-energy rate $\nu_C$ as the carrier common to both tiers. In the two-kernel amplitude, the two Dirac branches appear as the components $\pm\omZB/2$ of $\cos(\iphase/2)$, and Paper~\#65 relates the internal rate to the branch phase $m_ec^2/\hbar$ by the factor $\bZB$~\cite{H65}. Which rate the phase of a branch carries in the envelope picture is the question of the layer of the Dirac frequency (Section~\ref{sec:discrete}).

\paragraph{The envelope equation rests on covariance.} Section~\ref{sec:envelope} obtains the Klein--Gordon form from Lorentz covariance and the rest energy, both retained by the model, together with the Planck--de~Broglie relations and a linear single-component field, and the Schr\"odinger equation from it. What the model contributes is the identification of the factored-out carrier with an internal oscillation and the separation of the external envelope from the internal transfer. The internal tier itself is not shown to produce the dispersion relation; that would require a derivation of the rest energy $m_ec^2$ from the internal transfer.

\paragraph{The product structure of the energy.} The exact ratio two between the internal lines follows from the uniform advance of $\iphase$; writing the energy as the density of a product adds no constraint for identical factors. The identification of the two factors with physical constituents, the two kernels in particular, is not established, and the split, sum and beat lines of non-identical factors are stated as a consequence of the product reading only. That the Fourier components of the energy at $\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ and $2\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ are emitted or absorbed as lines is not shown.

\paragraph{The momentum identity.} The equality of the momentum of the internal line and of a body of mass $m_e$ moving at $v_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ holds for any $\bZB$ and carries no information about its value. Whether the vacuum momentum $\bZB m_ec$ or the medium momentum $m_ec$ enters the recoil balance is not decided here.

\section{Conclusion}

The 0-Sphere model had developed the ray half of Hamilton's analogy: thermal geodesics, an optical metric, and a phase accumulated along the arc between the kernels that serves as a principal function. Writing the internal wave in the eikonal form separates the phase, governed by the Hamilton--Jacobi equation and by the phase index, from the intensity, transported at the group velocity and governed by the group index. The two rows of the geodesic hierarchy of the series are the two lowest orders of this expansion. The group index $1/\bZB$ does not depend on the origin of energy; the phase index of a matter wave does. The closure of the internal cycle on a round trip of length $\lambdaC$ fixes the ratio of the two indices of the internal carrier, and a body of the electron's mass moving at the internal speed does not satisfy it. Factoring out the rest-energy oscillation leaves an envelope that obeys the Schr\"odinger equation, and the resulting two tiers, an external envelope that spreads and an internal transfer whose envelope is restored by the energy identity, correspond to the convective and spin terms of the Gordon decomposition. The internal tier lies outside Schr\"odinger dynamics, since a packet of its size doubles in less than half a cycle. For a non-dispersive carrier whose phase is counted with the internal phase $\iphase$, the dynamical phase accumulates $2\pi$ per round trip and the internal action is $J = h$; counted with the half angle of the amplitude it is $h/2$, and the choice between the two counts is left open. The uniform advance of the internal phase places the second Fourier component of the energy at exactly $41.36$~keV; written as the density of a product of two amplitudes, the energy shows that non-identical factors would split the first line and add sum and beat lines. The stability of the free electron requires that the internal transfer leave the centre of charge fixed.

\item[I1.] $\bZB^2 = 0.00163798087$ (Paper~\#10).
\item[I2.] The phase functional $\mathcal{S} = \Sdyn + \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{geo}}$ and the world function $\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}$ (Paper~\#51, version~3).
\item[I3.] The internal index $n = 1/\bZB$, the round trip of length $\lambdaC$, and the discrete spectrum $0$, $\omega$, $2\omega$ (Paper~\#66).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Results.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.4em]
\item[R1.] The eikonal expansion separates the phase (Hamilton--Jacobi, phase index) from the intensity (continuity at the group velocity, group index); the two rows of the geodesic hierarchy of Paper~\#51 are its two lowest orders.
\item[R2.] The group index $1/\bZB$ is independent of the origin of energy; the phase index of a matter wave is $\bZB$ with the rest energy kept and $2/\bZB$ with it removed.
\item[R3.] The closure of the internal cycle fixes the ratio $\nph/\ngr = N$ of the indices of the internal carrier; a body of mass $m_e$ moving at $v_{\mathrm{ZB}}$ does not close. For the non-dispersive lowest mode with its phase counted by $\iphase$, the dynamical phase accumulates $2\pi$ per round trip and $J = h$; counted by $\iphase/2$, $J = h/2$.
\item[R4.] Factoring out $\nu_C$ leaves an envelope obeying the Schr\"odinger equation, a heat equation with imaginary diffusivity $(\lambdaC/2)(c/2\pi)$.
\item[R5.] Two tiers: external envelope (continuous, spreading, convective current) and internal transfer (discrete, restored, spin current); a free Schr\"odinger packet of width $\lambdaC$ doubles in $0.44$ of an internal cycle.
\item[R6.] The uniform advance of $\iphase$ places the second Fourier component of the energy at exactly $2\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}$. Written as the density of a product of two amplitudes, non-identical factors would give lines at $\omega_A$, $\omega_B$, $\omega_A + \omega_B$ and $|\omega_A - \omega_B|$.
\item[R7.] The internal transfer may not oscillate the charge dipole; a free electron would otherwise lose its rest energy within $10^{-13}$~s.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Open items.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.4em]
\item[O1.] The count of the internal phase ($\iphase$, giving $J = h$, or $\iphase/2$, giving $J = h/2$) and the non-dispersive lowest mode, on which R3 rests.
\item[O2.] A derivation of the rest energy $m_ec^2$, and with it of the dispersion relation of the whole electron, from the internal transfer.
\item[O3.] The physical identity of the two factors of the product in R6, and whether a detuning of the kernels appears as the shifted and beat lines.
\item[O4.] The layer (bilinear or quartic) with which the Dirac frequency is to be compared, and the rate carried by the phase of a Dirac branch.
\item[O5.] The bound on an internal resonance near $20.7$~keV from existing Compton-scattering data.
\item[O6.] The recoil closure suggested by the momentum identity, including whether the vacuum or the medium momentum enters it.
\end{itemize}
