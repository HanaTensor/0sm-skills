% Abstract

\begin{abstract}
In general relativity the multipoles of a source play sharply different roles: the monopole is conserved, the mass dipole cannot radiate because its rate of change is the total momentum, and gravitational radiation begins at the quadrupole, at twice the orbital frequency.
This paper shows that the internal energy transfer of the 0-Sphere electron has the same hierarchy, and that each level follows from the energy identity $\cos^4(\theta/2) + \sin^4(\theta/2) + \tfrac12\sin^2\theta = 1$ of the model.
Resolved into frequencies, the identity balances separately in three channels: a constant (the conserved total), a fundamental $\omega$ that is antisymmetric in the two kernels (the exchange from one kernel to the other), and a second harmonic $2\omega$ that is symmetric (the exchange between the kernels and the photon sphere).
The fundamental moves the centre of energy; conservation of the mass dipole therefore forces the kernels to recoil, and the recoil closure $X(\theta) = \tfrac{d}{2}\cos\theta\,(1 + \tfrac12\sin^2\theta)$ cancels the dipole identically. The fundamental remains in the momentum of each element, and the cancellation holds for the total, which is the quantity to which gravitation couples; it requires the momentum carried by the transfer of energy between separated elements, and it requires the photon sphere to carry the momentum of a slow body, $Kv/c^2$, rather than the momentum $K/c$ of free light, in agreement with the group-velocity reading of the companion Letter.
The second moment that remains is $J(\theta) = E_0d^2\sin^4\theta\,(1 + \sin^2\theta)/16$, which contains only the even harmonics $2\omega$, $4\omega$ and $6\omega$ and no others.
Its amplitude is largest at $2\omega$; because quadrupole radiation weights each harmonic by the sixth power of its frequency, $79.11\%$ of the radiated power lies at $4\omega$ ($82.7$~keV).
This even-harmonic form replaces the quadrupole proposed in Paper \#58 of the series, which held the kernels fixed and oscillated at the fundamental.
For a core separation $\lambda_C/2$ the power is $6.3\times10^{-45}$~W, unobservable and harmless to stability; the result is structural rather than observational.
The paper addresses the form of the gravitational source, not its coupling strength, and records which parts of the correspondence with general relativity remain open. The kinematic results were reproduced independently, without access to them, by two blind verifications whose findings are recorded in an appendix.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A source of gravitational radiation in general relativity is characterized by its multipole moments, and the three lowest moments behave in three different ways~\cite{MTW,Maggiore}. The monopole, the total mass-energy, is conserved and does not radiate. The mass dipole $\bm{D} = \sum_i E_i\bm{x}_i/c^2$ does not radiate either, for a reason that has no counterpart in electrodynamics: its time derivative is the total momentum, $d\bm{D}/dt = \bm{P}$, which is conserved, so that $\ddot{\bm{D}} = 0$. Gravitational radiation therefore begins at the quadrupole. For a binary in a circular orbit, the quadrupole moment is a quadratic function of the positions, and it oscillates at twice the orbital frequency; the waves observed by the LIGO and Virgo detectors~\cite{LIGO2016} carry exactly this doubled frequency.

The 0-Sphere model describes the electron as two kernels, $A$ and $B$, and an amount of energy, the photon sphere, carried between them~\cite{H65,H51}. The companion Letter, Paper \#66~\cite{H66}, showed that the transfer is continuous in its course but discrete in its composition: resolved into frequencies, the energy identity of the model contains only a constant, a fundamental and a second harmonic. The present paper asks whether this discrete structure reproduces the multipole hierarchy of general relativity, and finds that it does at every level that can be computed without further assumptions.

The link between the internal motion of the 0-Sphere electron and quadrupole gravitational radiation is not new to the series. Paper \#58~\cite{H58} proposed that the energy distribution between the kernels carries a time-dependent quadrupole moment that radiates through the standard quadrupole formula, and Paper \#59~\cite{H59} asked how such a quadrupole could acquire a fixed direction for an electron bound to a proton. In Paper \#58 the moment was taken proportional to the energy difference of the kernels, $T_A - T_B = E_0\cos\theta$, with the kernels held fixed, so that it oscillated at the fundamental. The present paper derives the source side of that proposal from the energy identity and the conservation of the mass dipole alone. The result replaces the form of Paper \#58: holding the kernels fixed violates the conservation of the mass dipole (Sec.~\ref{sec:dipole}), the energy difference belongs to the antisymmetric channel, which is the dipole channel and is gravitationally silent, and the quadrupole that remains contains only even harmonics (Sec.~\ref{sec:quadrupole}). The single-frequency quadrupole of Paper \#58 is therefore superseded by Eq.~(\ref{eq:J}). The question of direction raised in Paper \#59 is not addressed here.

\paragraph{Synopsis.} The argument proceeds in five steps, each in its own section.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=2pt]
\item Section~\ref{sec:channels} resolves the energy identity into three channels, a constant, a fundamental $\omega$ and a second harmonic $2\omega$, and states the selection rule that removes odd harmonics from any quantity symmetric under the exchange of the kernels.
\item Section~\ref{sec:dipole} shows that fixed kernels violate the conservation of the mass dipole, derives the recoil that restores it, and, in Sec.~\ref{sec:bookkeeping}, checks the bookkeeping of momentum element by element.
\item Section~\ref{sec:quadrupole} computes the second moment that remains, its harmonics and the shares of the radiated power.
\item Section~\ref{sec:gr} sets the three channels beside the multipoles of a gravitational source in general relativity.
\item Section~\ref{sec:limits} states the limitations, and Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.
\end{itemize}
Appendix~\ref{app:numbers} reproduces the numbers, Appendix~\ref{app:blind} records the blind verification, and Appendix~\ref{app:interface} lists the inputs, results and open items.

\paragraph{Scope.} The paper concerns the form of the gravitational source of the 0-Sphere electron: which multipoles oscillate, at which frequencies, and with which weights. It does not define the gravitational charge, which the weak equivalence principle requires to be proportional to the total energy, and it does not derive the curvature side of the Einstein equations. The internal oscillation is taken as given; its origin is not addressed, and the identity describes its persistence. The word radiation is used for gravitational radiation and, where stated, for the internal transfer between the kernels; electromagnetic emission is not discussed. In particular, the recoiling kernels do not form an oscillating electric dipole: in the 0-Sphere model the charge is not located at the kernels but is a winding number carried by the system as a whole~\cite{H65}, and an electric dipole of the size of the core separation, of order $e\lambda_C \approx 2.4\times10^{-10}\,e$~cm, is excluded by the electron electric-dipole-moment bound $4.1\times10^{-30}\,e$~cm~\cite{Roussy2023} by twenty orders of magnitude.

\section{Three Channels of the Energy Identity}
\label{sec:channels}

\textbf{[standard]} With the internal phase $\theta$, the energies held at the kernels and in transit are
\begin{align}
T_A &= E_0\cos^4\frac{\theta}{2}, \qquad T_B = E_0\sin^4\frac{\theta}{2},
\notag\\
K &= \frac12E_0\sin^2\theta,
\label{eq:energies}
\end{align}
and $T_A + T_B + K = E_0$ for every $\theta$. The half-angle and double-angle formulas give~\cite{H66}
\begin{align}
\frac{T_A}{E_0} &= \frac38 + \frac12\cos\theta + \frac18\cos2\theta,
\notag\\
\frac{T_B}{E_0} &= \frac38 - \frac12\cos\theta + \frac18\cos2\theta,
\notag\\
\frac{K}{E_0} &= \frac14 - \frac14\cos2\theta.
\label{eq:fourier}
\end{align}
The phase advances at $\omega = 2\pi\nu_{\text{ZB}}$, with $\nu_{\text{ZB}} = \beta_{\text{ZB}}c/\lambda_C = 5.0007\times10^{18}$~Hz and $\beta_{\text{ZB}} = 0.040472$ fixed by the anomalous magnetic moment~\cite{H10,H62}.

Equation~(\ref{eq:fourier}) balances separately at each frequency, and each frequency is a separate channel.

\paragraph{The constant channel.} The constant parts sum to $\tfrac38 + \tfrac38 + \tfrac14 = 1$. This is the conserved total $E_0$, the monopole.

\paragraph{The fundamental channel, $\omega$.} The parts at $\omega$ are $+\tfrac12$ at $A$, $-\tfrac12$ at $B$ and $0$ in transit. They are antisymmetric under the exchange of the two kernels and describe the transfer from one kernel to the other. This channel moves the centre of energy along the axis.

\paragraph{The second-harmonic channel, $2\omega$.} The parts at $2\omega$ are $+\tfrac18$ at $A$, $+\tfrac18$ at $B$ and $-\tfrac14$ in transit. They are symmetric under the exchange of the kernels and describe the exchange between the kernels taken together and the photon sphere. This channel does not move the centre of energy; it changes how widely the energy is spread along the axis.

\textbf{[0SM reading]} The exchange of the kernels corresponds to the shift $\theta \to \theta + \pi$, under which $\cos k\theta$ changes sign for odd $k$ and is unchanged for even $k$. Any quantity that is symmetric under the exchange, such as a second moment of the energy distribution, can therefore contain only even harmonics of $\omega$. This selection rule, rather than any detail of the distribution, is what removes the odd harmonics from the quadrupole in Sec.~\ref{sec:quadrupole}.

% Section 3: The Dipole Channel Is Silent
% ========================================
\section{The Dipole Channel Is Silent}
\label{sec:dipole}

\paragraph{Fixed kernels are excluded.} \textbf{[standard]} For an isolated system at rest, the mass dipole is constant. Place the kernels at $\mp d/2$ on the axis and the photon sphere at $u(\theta)$. If the kernels were fixed, the condition $T_A(-d/2) + T_B(d/2) + K\,u = 0$ would require
\begin{equation}
\frac{u(\theta)}{d} = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta},
\label{eq:fixed}
\end{equation}
which diverges at $\theta = 0$ and $\theta = \pi$, the moments at which all the energy is at one kernel. The carrier would have to leave any bounded region. Fixed kernels are therefore excluded by the conservation of the mass dipole.

\paragraph{The recoil closure.} \textbf{[0SM reading]} Let the pair of kernels move together by $X(\theta)$, so that the kernels sit at $X \mp d/2$, and let the photon sphere sit at $X - \tfrac{d}{2}\cos\theta$, close to the kernel that holds the energy. The dipole condition
\begin{equation}
T_A\Bigl(X - \frac d2\Bigr) + T_B\Bigl(X + \frac d2\Bigr) + K\Bigl(X - \frac d2\cos\theta\Bigr) = 0
\label{eq:dipole}
\end{equation}
then fixes the recoil uniquely:
\begin{equation}
\frac{X(\theta)}{d} = \frac12\cos\theta\left(1 + \frac12\sin^2\theta\right).
\label{eq:recoil}
\end{equation}
With Eq.~(\ref{eq:recoil}) the dipole vanishes identically at every phase. The kernel that holds the energy sits at the centre of energy, and the pair swings through $\pm d/2$. The amplitude of this swing is the core separation itself, not a free parameter, and the swing is the internal counterpart of the Zitterbewegung~\cite{Schrodinger1930}.

The fundamental channel of Sec.~\ref{sec:channels}, which moves the centre of energy, is thereby compensated by the recoil of the kernels. In the language of general relativity, the 0-Sphere electron carries no time-dependent mass dipole, and its fundamental channel does not radiate gravitationally. This is the same conclusion, for the same reason, as the absence of dipole gravitational radiation in general relativity.

\paragraph{Speed of the recoil.} \textbf{[standard]} From Eq.~(\ref{eq:recoil}), $dX/dt = -\tfrac34\omega d\sin^3\theta$, so that the largest speed of the kernels is $\tfrac34\omega d$ at $\theta = \pi/2$. For the core separation $d = \lambda_C/2$ of the series this is $0.0954\,c$; for $d = \lambda_C$ it would be $0.191\,c$. The photon sphere moves at most at $\tfrac14\omega d$, $0.032\,c$ for $d = \lambda_C/2$. All speeds are below $c$.

% Subsection 3.1: Momentum Bookkeeping

\subsection{Momentum Bookkeeping}
\label{sec:bookkeeping}

The vanishing of the dipole is imposed in Eq.~(\ref{eq:dipole}), and in that sense it is true by construction. A test that is not automatic is obtained by assigning a momentum to each element and asking whether the assignments are consistent. Differentiating the dipole gives
\begin{equation}
c^2\frac{dD}{dt} = \sum_i E_i\,\dot x_i + \sum_i \dot E_i\,x_i ,
\label{eq:bookkeeping}
\end{equation}
where the first sum is the momentum of the elements as moving bodies and the second is the momentum carried by the transfer of energy between separated places, the energy current divided by $c^2$. Evaluated on the closure~(\ref{eq:recoil}), the two parts are
\begin{align}
\sum_i E_i\,\dot x_i &= -\frac{E_0\omega d}{2}\sin^3\theta,
\notag\\
\sum_i \dot E_i\,x_i &= +\frac{E_0\omega d}{2}\sin^3\theta .
\label{eq:twoparts}
\end{align}
Three conclusions follow.

\paragraph{The silence is a silence of the total.} The momentum of the pair of kernels, the momentum of the photon sphere and the force on the pair each contain the fundamental $\omega$, together with $3\omega$ and $5\omega$. Only their sum, with the transfer term included, vanishes. Gravitation couples to the total energy and therefore sees only the total, so the conclusion of Sec.~\ref{sec:dipole} stands; the two stars of a binary likewise move at the orbital frequency while the binary emits no dipole radiation. The statement is nonetheless conditional on the bookkeeping: if one element is assigned a different momentum, a dipole at $\omega$ reappears.

\paragraph{The transfer carries momentum.} Omitting the transfer term and requiring the momenta of the bodies alone to vanish gives a different recoil, under which the dipole oscillates and $dD/dt = P$ is violated. A placement of the photon sphere for which the transfer term vanishes, so that energy is handed over only where the photon sphere is, requires $x_\gamma - X = -(d/2)/\cos\theta$, which lies outside the kernels and diverges at $\theta = \pi/2$. The momentum of the transfer cannot be avoided in a model of point-like elements.

\paragraph{The photon sphere moves as a slow body.} If the photon sphere is assigned the momentum of free light, $K/c$, the bookkeeping fails: that momentum exceeds $Kv_\gamma/c^2$ by an order of magnitude or more, and restoring zero total momentum would require the kernels to move faster than light. The bookkeeping closes only if the photon sphere carries the momentum $Kv_\gamma/c^2$ of a body of invariant mass $K/c^2$ moving well below $c$, closer to a confined standing wave than to free light. This is the reading of the companion Letter, Paper \#66~\cite{H66}, in which the internal carrier propagates at a group velocity $c/n$ with $n \approx 25$; the form $Kv/c^2$ is also the form of the Abraham momentum of light in a medium.

\section{The Quadrupole Channel}
\label{sec:quadrupole}

With the dipole removed, the lowest moment that can oscillate is the second moment of the energy along the axis,
\begin{equation}
J(\theta) = T_A\Bigl(X - \frac d2\Bigr)^2 + T_B\Bigl(X + \frac d2\Bigr)^2 + K\Bigl(X - \frac d2\cos\theta\Bigr)^2.
\label{eq:Jdef}
\end{equation}
Substituting Eqs.~(\ref{eq:energies}) and~(\ref{eq:recoil}) gives the closed form
\begin{align}
\frac{J(\theta)}{E_0d^2} &= \frac{\sin^4\theta\,(1 + \sin^2\theta)}{16}
\notag\\
&= \frac{22 - 31\cos2\theta + 10\cos4\theta - \cos6\theta}{512}.
\label{eq:J}
\end{align}
Three features of Eq.~(\ref{eq:J}) carry the argument.

\paragraph{Only even harmonics.} The second moment contains $2\omega$, $4\omega$ and $6\omega$ and no odd harmonic, as the selection rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:channels} requires.

\paragraph{A finite spectrum.} The spectrum stops at $6\omega$. The energies~(\ref{eq:energies}) are polynomials of degree two in $\cos\theta$, and the positions are polynomials of degree three, so that their products in Eq.~(\ref{eq:Jdef}) cannot contain a frequency above $6\omega$. As in Paper \#66, the number of lines is fixed by the degrees of the polynomials, and their weights by the identity.

\paragraph{Amplitude and power peak at different harmonics.} The amplitude is largest at $2\omega$ ($31/512$), smaller at $4\omega$ ($10/512$) and smallest at $6\omega$ ($1/512$). The power radiated by a quadrupole depends on the third time derivative of the moment, and therefore weights the $k$-th harmonic by $k^6$. The shares of the power are
\begin{align}
2\omega:&\quad 11.88\%,
\notag\\
4\omega:&\quad 79.11\%,
\notag\\
6\omega:&\quad 9.01\%,
\label{eq:shares}
\end{align}
so that the dominant line of the radiated power is $4\omega$, at $4\nu_{\text{ZB}} = 2.00\times10^{19}$~Hz ($82.7$~keV). The mean square of the third derivative of Eq.~(\ref{eq:J}) with respect to $\theta$ is exactly $4045/4096$.

\paragraph{The size of the effect.} \textbf{[standard]} For a moment $I(t) = M d^2 J(\omega t)/(E_0d^2)$ along one axis, the traceless quadrupole has $\sum_{ij}Q_{ij}^2 = \tfrac23 I^2$, and the quadrupole formula gives
\begin{equation}
P = \frac{G}{5c^5}\,\frac23\,M^2d^4\omega^6\,\frac{4045}{4096}.
\label{eq:P}
\end{equation}
With $M = m_e$ and the core separation $d = \lambda_C/2$ of the series, $P = 6.3\times10^{-45}$~W; with $d = \lambda_C$ the power would be sixteen times larger, $1.00\times10^{-43}$~W, because $P \propto d^4$. Over the age of the universe the smaller value removes $1.7\times10^{-8}$~eV from an electron of rest energy $511$~keV. The internal oscillation is stable against gravitational radiation by a wide margin, and the radiation itself is far beyond any conceivable detection. The value of the result lies in its structure.

\paragraph{The origin of the doubled frequency.} In general relativity the quadrupole of a binary oscillates at twice the orbital frequency because the moment is quadratic in the positions. In the 0-Sphere model the second harmonic first appears in the energies themselves, because the energy at a kernel is the square of its share~\cite{H65,H66}, and the positions add further even harmonics through the second moment. Both doublings arise from a quadratic form. Whether they are one mechanism seen from two sides, or two mechanisms that happen to agree, is not settled here.

\paragraph{What the correspondence supplies.} The open question of the series on the gravitational sector has been how a rank-two source can arise from the internal structure of the electron~\cite{H58,H59}. The correspondence supplies the source side of that question with definite content: the source oscillates only at even harmonics of the internal frequency, its spectrum is finite and fixed in advance, its amplitudes are given by Eq.~(\ref{eq:J}), and the dipole that would otherwise dominate is removed by a conservation law rather than by an assumption.

% Section 6: Limitations
% ========================================
\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{A point-carrier model.} The photon sphere is placed at a single point, $X - \tfrac d2\cos\theta$. The amplitudes of Eq.~(\ref{eq:J}) depend on this choice. If the photon sphere is spread uniformly between the kernels, the recoil contains $\omega$ alone and the second moment $J = \tfrac16E_0d^2\sin^2\theta$ contains $2\omega$ alone; if it travels between the kernels at constant speed, the recoil contains all odd harmonics and the second moment all even harmonics. The finite spectrum ending at $6\omega$ and the shares~(\ref{eq:shares}) are therefore properties of the placement used here, not of the model as a whole. What does not depend on the placement is the existence and uniqueness of the recoil, its amplitude $d/2$, and the absence of odd harmonics in the second moment, which follows whenever the placement respects the exchange of the kernels, $g(\theta + \pi) = -g(\theta)$ for $x_\gamma = X + \tfrac d2g(\theta)$.

\paragraph{The energy budget in transit.} Energy moved between separated points at a speed $u \le c$ occupies the path while it travels. With the rate of transfer fixed by the identity, the energy in transit is at least $(\omega d/2c)E_0$ at $\theta = \pi/2$, which is $6.4\%$ of $E_0$ for $d = \lambda_C/2$, whatever the placement. The three terms of the identity do not contain this energy, and the energy budget of the point-element model therefore fails to close at order $\omega d/c$. The kinetic energy of the recoil, at most $0.25\%$ of $E_0$ for $d = \lambda_C/2$, also lies outside the budget. A model in which the photon sphere is a continuous distribution with an energy density $u$ and an energy current $S$ satisfying $\partial_tu + \partial_xS = 0$ locally is required to remove these discrepancies.

\paragraph{Kinematics without a force law.} The recoil~(\ref{eq:recoil}) is the motion that the conservation of the mass dipole requires; it is not obtained from an equation of motion. The paper shows that this motion is possible, bounded and subluminal, not that the kernels are driven to perform it. An equation of motion for the kernels would allow Eq.~(\ref{eq:recoil}) to be tested as a solution.

\paragraph{Validity of the quadrupole formula.} The quadrupole formula assumes a source small compared with the reduced wavelength of the radiation. The relevant parameter is $k\omega d/c$ for the $k$-th harmonic, which for $d = \lambda_C/2$ is $0.25$, $0.51$ and $0.76$ at $2\omega$, $4\omega$ and $6\omega$. The shares~(\ref{eq:shares}) and the power~(\ref{eq:P}) are therefore estimates whose corrections from higher multipoles and retardation may reach tens of percent at the dominant harmonic; for $d = \lambda_C$ the parameter reaches unity at $4\omega$.

\paragraph{The recoil and the internal index.} The largest speed of the kernels, $\tfrac34\omega d$, exceeds the group velocity $c/n = \beta_{\text{ZB}}c$ of the internal carrier in Paper \#66~\cite{H66}. For $d = \lambda_C/2$ the ratio is $3\pi/4 = 2.36$, independent of $\beta_{\text{ZB}}$, and the kernels move faster than $c/n$ during $46\%$ of each cycle, wherever $|\sin\theta|^3 > 4/(3\pi)$. If the phase index of the internal region equals its group index, as the critical angle of Paper \#66 assumes, the kernels then move faster than the phase velocity of the carrier, which is the condition for Cherenkov-type emission into the internal region; its consequences for the transfer are not examined here. If instead the phase velocity stays close to $c$ and only the group velocity is reduced, as for a mode confined in a waveguide, the condition does not arise. Which case holds is the relation between the phase index and the group index, left open in Paper \#66.

\paragraph{The curvature side.} The paper derives which moments of the source oscillate. It does not derive the field equations, and the association of the channels with the Ricci and Weyl parts of the curvature in Table~\ref{tab:corr} is a correspondence, not a derivation.

\paragraph{The coupling strength.} The form of the source and the strength of its coupling are separate questions. The gravitational charge must be the total energy alone, independent of the internal speed, and the present paper does not address how the model meets that requirement.

\paragraph{The scale of the energy.} Equation~(\ref{eq:P}) takes $E_0 = m_ec^2$ and $d = \lambda_C/2$. Other identifications of the energy scale in the series rescale $P$ but leave the harmonic structure and the shares~(\ref{eq:shares}) unchanged. If the kernels carry a rest energy of their own in addition to $E_0$, the amplitude and the speed of the recoil are reduced in proportion, and the shares change.

% ========================================
% Section 7: Conclusion
% ========================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The energy identity of the 0-Sphere model, resolved into frequencies, separates into three channels with the roles of the three lowest multipoles of a gravitational source. The constant channel is the conserved total. The fundamental channel, the exchange from one kernel to the other, moves the centre of energy, and the conservation of the mass dipole forces the kernels to recoil in exactly the way that cancels it, so that the channel is gravitationally silent. What remains is a quadrupole that oscillates only at even harmonics of the internal frequency, with a finite spectrum ending at $6\omega$, its amplitude largest at $2\omega$ and its radiated power dominated by $4\omega$. The 0-Sphere electron, in other words, begins at the quadrupole for the same reason that gravitational radiation in general relativity does. The silence of the dipole is a silence of the total momentum, which requires the momentum of the transfer and a photon sphere that moves as a slow body; the remaining discrepancies in the energy budget are of order $\omega d/c$ and point to a continuous description of the photon sphere as the next step.

\section{Blind Verification}
\label{app:blind}

The kinematic content of Secs.~\ref{sec:dipole}--\ref{sec:quadrupole} was checked by two independent verifications carried out before the results above were shown to them. Each verifier received the same brief, which stated the energies~(\ref{eq:energies}), the placement of the kernels and of the photon sphere, the conservation of the mass dipole, and six questions, and which contained none of the results. Each worked in a separate folder containing only the brief, with no access to the web, to earlier conversations or to stored memory, and wrote its report and scripts into that folder.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item Verifier A: Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), reasoning effort medium, run in Claude Code on 13 November 2026, about ten minutes.
\item Verifier B: Claude Fable 5.2 (Anthropic), reasoning effort medium, run in Claude Code on 13 November 2026, about sixteen minutes.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Agreement.} Both verifiers obtained, independently and by different routes, the oscillation of the dipole for fixed kernels, the recoil~(\ref{eq:recoil}) in the form $\tfrac d{16}(9\cos\theta - \cos3\theta)$, its uniqueness up to a constant, its amplitude $d/2$ and largest speed $\tfrac34\omega d$, the closed form~(\ref{eq:J}), the absence of odd harmonics, the shares~(\ref{eq:shares}) and the power~(\ref{eq:P}). Verifier A solved the problem symbolically and confirmed it numerically; verifier B combined symbolic and numerical work and changed from symbolic integration to a fast Fourier transform when the former proved too slow.

\paragraph{Points raised.} The verifiers raised the points that are now stated in Sec.~\ref{sec:bookkeeping} and Sec.~\ref{sec:limits}: that the vanishing of the dipole is true by construction and that the non-trivial test is the bookkeeping of momentum (both); that the fundamental survives in the momentum of each element (both); that the momentum $K/c$ of free light is incompatible with the bookkeeping (both); that the energy in transit lies outside the budget at order $\omega d/c$ (A), and that the kinetic energy of the recoil does as well (B); that the quadrupole formula is at the edge of its validity for $d = \lambda_C$ (A); that the recoil is kinematic and lacks a force law (B); and that the result depends on the placement of the photon sphere (both). Verifier B also noted that an electric charge located at the kernels would produce an oscillating electric dipole; the Scope paragraph of Sec.~I records why the 0-Sphere model does not place the charge there. The comparison also exposed an inconsistency in an earlier draft of this paper, which used $d = \lambda_C$ for the power while taking the core separation to be $\lambda_C/2$; the present version uses $\lambda_C/2$ throughout. The reports and scripts of both verifiers are deposited with this paper.

\section{Inputs, Results and Open Items}

\item[R4.] The second moment~(\ref{eq:J}) contains only $2\omega$, $4\omega$, $6\omega$; amplitude peaks at $2\omega$, radiated power at $4\omega$ ($79.11\%$, $82.7$~keV).

\item[R6.] The hierarchy monopole, silent dipole, quadrupole coincides with that of a gravitational source in general relativity (Table~\ref{tab:corr}).
\item[R7.] The momentum bookkeeping closes only with the momentum of the transfer, Eq.~(\ref{eq:twoparts}), and only if the photon sphere carries the momentum $Kv/c^2$ of a slow body, consistent with the group velocity $c/n$ of Paper \#66.
\item[R8.] The kinematic results were reproduced by two blind verifications (Appendix~\ref{app:blind}).
\item[R9.] The single-frequency quadrupole of Paper \#58, proportional to $T_A - T_B$ with fixed kernels, is superseded by Eq.~(\ref{eq:J}) (Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}).
\end{itemize}
