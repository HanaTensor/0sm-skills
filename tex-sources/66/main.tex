\begin{abstract}
The paper has two parts.
Part~I is a step-by-step account of Paper~\#65, which builds the electron of the 0-Sphere model from the arithmetic of $0$ and $1$: the energy split between two kernels and the transit term, the segment and its two ends, charge as a protected winding number whose integer follows and whose size does not, the quartic energy as a quadratic form seen through the double cover, the Dirac branches read as the radiating and absorbing halves of one exchange, and an internal clock fixed by the anomalous magnetic moment; it includes the choice of the Compton wavelength as the transport length made in Paper~\#33.
Part~II applies that clock to the weak equivalence principle.
The weak equivalence principle says that free fall does not depend on mass, on internal binding or on composition.
Paper~\#52 of the 0-Sphere series obtained the first of the three: the Compton wavelength enters the force twice, as lever arm and as inverse rate, and cancels, leaving $F = mg\,\betaZB$ with $\betaZB = \vZB/c = 0.040472$ the average speed at which the electron's energy is carried between its two kernels.
This paper tests the other two clauses, with the internal frequency set throughout at the subluminal value of Paper~\#10, $\nuZB = \betaZB c/\lambdaC = 5.0007\times10^{18}$~Hz.
Binding passes exactly.
For a Dirac--Coulomb bound electron the cancellation returns $F = (E/c^{2})\,g\,\betaZB$, and the Thomas vortex reduces to $\OmT = (\vZB^{3}/4\hbar c^{3})E$, both strictly linear in the total energy, because $\betaZB$ is a pure number fixed by the anomalous magnetic moment.
A standard identity, $\expval{\beta} = E/mc^{2}$ for every Dirac--Coulomb state, verified by direct integration of the radial equations, equates the negative-energy admixture of such a state with half its binding-energy defect.
Composition fails.
The same bridge equation that fixes $\betaZB$ assigns $0.0405$ to leptons and $0.898$ to nucleons, so the force law makes titanium and platinum fall differently by $\eta = 3.13\times10^{-5}$, ten orders of magnitude above the MICROSCOPE bound, and a charge non-linear in energy fails by a still larger margin through nuclear binding.
The residual factor itself has a plain reading: $\betaZB = \nuZB/\nuC$, the ratio of the subluminal transport frequency to the light-speed frequency $c/\lambdaC$, so the force counts transport cycles where Newtonian free fall would need one count per Compton cycle, and the missing factor differs between species.
A local reading of the horizon freezing of Paper~\#63 requires the anomalous moment to vary with the gravitational potential at order unity, which atomic clock comparisons exclude by eight orders of magnitude.
The four failures share one cause: a quantity tied by the bridge equation to the anomalous moment has been asked to carry a charge that must be the total energy alone.
The mediator-construction route therefore cannot define the gravitational charge, and the formalisation is restarted from the line-integral papers \#29--\#31 under four acceptance criteria.
Three corrections to Papers~\#58 and~\#59 are recorded.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ========================================

A hydrogen atom weighs less than a free proton and a free electron. The difference is $13.6$~eV$/c^{2}$, the binding energy divided by $c^{2}$, and it is a real reduction in what the atom weighs on a balance and in how strongly it attracts other matter. Every form of energy gravitates, and gravitates equally per unit energy. This is the content of the weak equivalence principle (WEP), and a theory that proposes a microscopic origin for gravity has to reproduce it in all three of its clauses: free fall is independent of the mass of the body, of the energy that binds it, and of what it is made of.

\paragraph{Motivation.} The 0-Sphere model gives the electron an internal structure: two kernels, separated by the Compton wavelength $\lambdaC = h/mc$, between which the electron's energy is carried back and forth~\cite{hanamura01,hanamura10,hanamura65}. Paper~\#52~\cite{hanamura52} used this structure to derive the first clause. In a gravitational field the two kernels sit at different potentials, the energy carried between them picks up a small asymmetry on every cycle, and the force is that asymmetry times the cycle rate. The Compton wavelength enters once as the lever arm and once, inversely, through the rate, and cancels. The force comes out proportional to $m$ for any $m$, but with a residual factor, $F = mg\,\betaZB$, where $\betaZB = \vZB/c = 0.040472$ is the average transport speed in units of $c$. Paper~\#52 recorded the recovery of the full $F = mg$ as an open task. The other two clauses were not examined.

\paragraph{What this paper does.} It has two parts. Part~I (Sec.~\ref{sec:guide}) gives a step-by-step account of Paper~\#65~\cite{hanamura65}, on which everything that follows rests: how the model builds the electron from the arithmetic of $0$ and $1$, why charge is read as a winding number, how the quartic energy becomes a quadratic form, how the Dirac branches are read, and how the anomalous magnetic moment fixes an internal clock. Part~II applies the remaining two clauses of the equivalence principle as tests, under the frequency convention of Paper~\#10~\cite{hanamura10}: the internal cycle runs at the subluminal transport rate $\nuZB = \betaZB c/\lambdaC = 5.0007\times10^{18}$~Hz, and the light-speed value $\nuC = c/\lambdaC = 1.2356\times10^{20}$~Hz appears only as a reference. Figure~\ref{fig:roadmap} shows how the three clauses, the two tests and the conclusions fit together.

\paragraph{Binding.} A bound electron should gravitate by its reduced total energy. Inside the model this is not automatic: the electron's coupling is generated by an internal cycle whose size and rate are set by its energy, and binding changes that energy. Section~\ref{sec:bound} redoes the cancellation of Paper~\#52 for a Dirac--Coulomb bound electron from hydrogen to hydrogen-like uranium, where the electron carries only $74\%$ of its rest mass. The force comes out as $(E/c^{2})\,g\,\betaZB$ and the Thomas vortex of Paper~\#58~\cite{hanamura58} as a quantity strictly linear in $E$. The defect is carried exactly, and the reason is specific: $\betaZB$ is fixed by the anomalous magnetic moment and is a pure number, so binding cannot move it.

\paragraph{Composition.} Bodies of different composition should fall alike. Here the value of $\betaZB$ matters, and Paper~\#61~\cite{hanamura61} applies the same bridge equation to the proton and obtains $\betaZB^{p} = 0.898$. Section~\ref{sec:composition} shows that the force law then makes electrons and nucleons fall at rates differing by a factor of $22$, which gives bulk matter a composition-dependent free fall ten orders of magnitude above the MICROSCOPE bound~\cite{microscope2022}. The missing factor of Paper~\#52 is $24.7$ for a lepton and $1.11$ for a nucleon; no constant can supply it, and the coupling cannot contain $\betaZB$ at all.

\paragraph{The residual factor read as a ratio of frequencies.} With the convention of Paper~\#10, $\betaZB = \nuZB/\nuC$ identically. The force of Paper~\#52 counts one momentum transfer per transport cycle; Newtonian free fall would need the same transfer once per Compton cycle. The shortfall is the ratio of the two clocks, and the ratio is a different number for each species. Section~\ref{subsec:constraint} turns this into a precise requirement on any completion of Paper~\#52.

\paragraph{Further results.} Section~\ref{sec:theorems} records two exact statements about Dirac--Coulomb bound states, one of which equates the negative-energy admixture of a bound state with half its binding-energy defect. Section~\ref{sec:tension} shows that Papers~\#62~\cite{hanamura62} and~\#63~\cite{hanamura63} make incompatible demands on whether $\vZB$ is a local invariant, and that clock comparisons decide between them. Section~\ref{sec:diagnosis} collects the failures into one diagnosis, separates the rank-2 question of the spin-2 sector into its two halves, and states the restart. Section~\ref{sec:corrections} records three corrections to Papers~\#58 and~\#59~\cite{hanamura59}.

\paragraph{Two kinds of statement.} Statements of standard physics or mathematics are marked \textbf{[standard]}, and statements that belong to the interpretation of the 0-Sphere model are marked \textbf{[0SM reading]}, so that a reader can accept the first kind and still reject the second. Part~I and Section~\ref{sec:primer} assemble the background from the series, so that the paper can be read without it.

\subsection{Three Layers, Kept Apart Throughout}
\label{subsec:g_layers}

Paper~\#65 contains three kinds of statement, and Table~\ref{tab:layers} separates them. The first layer is mathematics: trigonometric identities, the boundary of a segment, the winding number of a closed curve, the map from a spinor to a Bloch vector, the arithmetic that turns a measured anomalous moment into a speed. These conclusions depend on the model's definitions. The third layer is what must be decided by experiment: whether the electron has two kernels, whether the internal transport is real, whether the predicted speed $0.04c$ is there. In the text these layers appear as \textbf{[standard]} for the first and \textbf{[0SM reading]} for the second; the third is the subject of Sec.~\ref{subsec:g_open}.

\subsection{The Picture of the Electron}
\label{subsec:g_picture}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} In the standard theory the electron is an elementary particle with no internal parts. The 0-Sphere model instead describes it by three elements: a kernel $A$, a kernel $B$, and the energy in transit between them, which the series calls the photon sphere. The two kernels are separated by the Compton wavelength $\lambdaC = h/mc = 2.43\times10^{-12}$~m. The model distinguishes three situations: the energy held at $A$, the energy held at $B$, and the energy on its way from one to the other. Keeping the third situation as a separate entry is what distinguishes the model's bookkeeping, and every later step depends on it.

The name of the model comes from mathematics. A sphere of dimension zero is the set of points at unit distance from the origin of the real line, $S^{0} = \{-1,+1\}$: two points and nothing between them. The model assigns these two points to the two kernels. The existence of such a structure inside the electron is an assumption of the model, not an observation.

\subsection{The Arithmetic Seed: Zero, One and the Two Signs}
\label{subsec:g_seed}

\textbf{[standard]} The paper begins with arithmetic. The integers are generated by the number $1$: every integer is a sum of ones or of their negatives. Two further facts single out the numbers $0$ and $1$ and the two signs. First, the numbers that do not change when squared satisfy $n^{2} = n$, that is $n(n-1) = 0$, so they are exactly $0$ and $1$. Every number strictly between them shrinks when squared: $0.5^{2} = 0.25$, $0.2^{2} = 0.04$. Only the two ends stay where they are. Second, the integers with an integer inverse are exactly $+1$ and $-1$, which is the set $S^{0}$ again.

\textbf{[0SM reading]} The model carries these two facts into the energy of the electron. The fraction of the energy held at each kernel is
\begin{equation}
n_A = \cos^{2}\frac{\iphase}{2}, \quad n_B = \sin^{2}\frac{\iphase}{2}, \quad n_A + n_B = 1,
\label{eq:g_occ}
\end{equation}
so that the two fractions always add to the whole. A moment with $n_A = 0.7$ and $n_B = 0.3$ is a moment at which $A$ holds $70\%$ and $B$ holds $30\%$. As the internal phase $\iphase$ advances, the share at $A$ falls and the share at $B$ rises, and then the flow reverses. These occupations belong to the internal model and are not the occupation numbers of quantum field theory.

\subsection{Why the Energy Carries a Fourth Power}
\label{subsec:g_quartic}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} The energy held at each kernel is defined as the square of its share,
\begin{align}
T_A &= E_0\,n_A^{2} = E_0\cos^{4}\frac{\iphase}{2},
\notag\\
T_B &= E_0\,n_B^{2} = E_0\sin^{4}\frac{\iphase}{2},
\label{eq:g_TATB}
\end{align}
with $E_0$ the energy scale of the whole system. Written with the amplitudes $a = \cos(\iphase/2)$ and $b = \sin(\iphase/2)$, the occupations are $n_A = a^{2}$, $n_B = b^{2}$ and the energies $T_A = E_0 a^{4}$, $T_B = E_0 b^{4}$. The construction has two steps: squaring the amplitude gives the occupation, and squaring the occupation gives the energy. This is the origin of the ``quartic energy'' in the title of Paper~\#65.

The two kernel energies do not add up to $E_0$. The shortfall is the energy in transit, and the identity that closes the books is
\begin{align}
1 &\;=\; \cos^{4}\frac{\iphase}{2} + \sin^{4}\frac{\iphase}{2} + \frac12\sin^{2}\iphase,
\notag\\
E_0 &\;=\; T_A + T_B + K,
\label{eq:g_identity}
\end{align}
where $K = \tfrac12 E_0\sin^{2}\iphase$ is read as the kinetic energy of the photon sphere in transit. The identity is layer~1; the reading of its three terms as energy at $A$, energy at $B$ and energy in transit is layer~2.

\paragraph{Four moments of one transport.} Table~\ref{tab:g_states} and Fig.~\ref{fig:g_split} follow the split through one transport from $A$ to $B$. At $\iphase = 0$ everything is at $A$ and nothing moves. At $\iphase = \pi/2$ each kernel holds a quarter and half of the energy is in transit: the kernels together hold only half, and the transit term carries the rest. At $\iphase = 2\pi/3$ the shares are $1/16$, $9/16$ and $6/16$, which again add to one. At $\iphase = \pi$ everything is at $B$, and the transport has stopped again. The place where the energy sits has moved from $A$ to $B$, and the transit term rose from zero to its maximum and fell back.

\subsection{A Segment and Its Two Ends}
\label{subsec:g_segment}

\textbf{[standard]} Section~III of Paper~\#65 reads the same normalisation at two degrees. At first degree, $n_A + n_B = 1$ with both shares non-negative is solved by every point of the segment from $(1,0)$ to $(0,1)$ in the plane of $(n_A,n_B)$: $(1,0)$, $(0.75,0.25)$, $(0.5,0.5)$ and every point between. This solution set is the segment $D^{1} = [0,1]$. At second degree, the kernels alone carry the whole when $n_A^{2} + n_B^{2} = 1$. Substituting $n_B = 1 - n_A$ gives $2n_A^{2} - 2n_A = 0$, that is $n_A(n_A - 1) = 0$, so only $n_A = 0$ and $n_A = 1$: the two ends of the segment. In the language of topology the two ends are the boundary of the segment,
\begin{equation}
\partial D^{1} \;=\; S^{0}.
\label{eq:g_boundary}
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:g_segment} draws both solution sets. The line $n_A + n_B = 1$ and the circle $n_A^{2} + n_B^{2} = 1$ meet exactly at the two ends.

\textbf{[0SM reading]} Paper~\#65 reads the first-degree normalisation as the boson side and the second-degree kernel terms as the fermion side: for a boson the normalisation holds on the whole segment, while for the fermion the kernels alone carry the whole only at its two ends, and between them the cross term $2n_An_B$ carries the energy in transit. This is a reading of the model's energy identity in topological language. It is not a proof of the spin--statistics theorem, and Paper~\#65 says so.

\subsection{Joining Two Separated Points}
\label{subsec:g_path}

\textbf{[standard]} A set is path-connected when any two of its points can be joined by a continuous path that stays inside it. The two-point set $S^{0} = \{-1,+1\}$ is not: a path from $-1$ to $+1$ must pass through points that are not in the set. The segment $D^{1}$ is path-connected, since every intermediate point belongs to it.

\textbf{[0SM reading]} If the kernels are two separated points, the energy cannot hop between them inside the two-point set. Paper~\#65 therefore distinguishes the set of kernel positions, which is $S^{0}$, from a larger connected domain that contains both and in which the transport runs. The kernels are discrete positions; the transport follows a continuous path between them. The geometry of that larger domain is taken from Papers~\#33 and~\#51~\cite{hanamura51}, not derived in Paper~\#65.

\paragraph{The first layer of the Helmholtz decomposition.} A vector field can, under suitable conditions, be split into a gradient part, a rotational part and a harmonic part, $\vb{u} = \nabla\phi + \nabla\times\bm{\psi} + \vb{h}$. Paper~\#65 uses the first of these. The normalised energy difference between the kernels is
\begin{align}
D &\;=\; \frac{T_A - T_B}{E_0} \;=\; \cos\iphase ,
\notag\\
\frac12\left(\frac{dD}{d\iphase}\right)^{2} &\;=\; \frac12\sin^{2}\iphase \;=\; \frac{K}{E_0},
\label{eq:g_gradient}
\end{align}
so the transit term is the square of the rate of change of the energy difference: the difference drives the transport. At this stage the internal phase is not yet a spatial coordinate, and the identification of gradients and curls in $\iphase$ with fields in space waits for a distance and a metric. A further caution of the paper belongs here: a vanishing curl guarantees a global potential only on a domain without holes, so the topology of the domain matters. That caution is the entrance to the next step.

\subsection{Closing the Path: Winding Numbers and Charge}
\label{subsec:g_winding}

\textbf{[standard]} Joining the two ends of the segment produces a circle, $D^{1}/\partial D^{1} \simeq S^{1}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:g_segment}b). Moving along a segment and going round a circle are different in an essential way: a path round the circle cannot be shrunk to a point without leaving the circle. The closed paths on a circle are classified by an integer, the number of times they go round, $\pi_1(S^{1}) = \mathbb{Z}$: once, twice, once in the opposite sense, or not at all.

\paragraph{A worked example from fluid mechanics.} The point vortex makes this concrete. In two dimensions its velocity field is $u_\vartheta = \Gamma/2\pi r$. Away from the centre the field has no curl, yet its integral round any closed curve that encloses the centre is
\begin{equation}
\oint_C \vb{u}\cdot d\vb{l} \;=\; \Gamma .
\label{eq:g_vortex}
\end{equation}
Locally the flow has a potential; globally the potential cannot be single-valued, because going once round the centre adds $\Gamma$. The information sits in the path round the hole, not in the field at any one point. In superfluids this circulation comes in integer multiples of $h/m$, and in superconductors the magnetic flux comes in integer multiples of $h/2e$: integers produced by closed paths are measured physics.

\paragraph{The amplitude plane.} \textbf{[standard]} Paper~\#65 combines the two kernel amplitudes into one complex number, $z = a + ib$, which moves on the unit circle because $\lvert z\rvert^{2} = a^{2} + b^{2} = 1$. The number of times a closed path of $z$ goes round the origin is
\begin{equation}
W \;=\; \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint \frac{a\,\dot b - b\,\dot a}{a^{2} + b^{2}}\,dt .
\label{eq:g_W}
\end{equation}
For $a = \cos t$, $b = \sin t$ the numerator is $\cos^{2}t + \sin^{2}t = 1$ and $W = 1$; going the other way gives $W = -1$. The winding number does not depend on the detailed shape of the path, only on how many times and in which sense it goes round. For the locked, normalised kernel pair, Paper~\#65 writes the integrand as the Wronskian
\begin{equation}
Q \;=\; \frac{2}{\omega}\left(a\,\dot b - b\,\dot a\right) \;=\; \pm1 ,
\label{eq:g_Q}
\end{equation}
which is conserved (Abel's theorem), quantised ($\pm1$) and bounded, $\lvert Q\rvert \le 1$ (the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality).

\paragraph{The reading as charge.} \textbf{[0SM reading]} Paper~\#65 identifies the sign of this winding with the sign of electric charge. Table~\ref{tab:g_charge} sets the correspondence out. The existence of charge corresponds to a closed path round the origin, its integer character to the integer winding, its conservation to the fact that no continuous change avoiding the origin alters the winding, its sign to the sense of rotation, and its bound to the normalisation. Standard electrodynamics expresses charge conservation through the continuity equation; the model proposes a different, internal origin for the same properties, and whether the correspondence describes the real electron is a layer-3 question.

\paragraph{The integer and its size.} \textbf{[0SM reading]} A winding of $\pm1$ fixes the sign and the integer character of the charge but not its magnitude in coulombs. Paper~\#65 states explicitly that the fine-structure constant $\alpha \approx 1/137.036$ is not derived, and that the coupling of the winding to the electromagnetic potential $A_\mu$ is open. Explaining the sign and the integer is one problem; obtaining the strength of electromagnetism is another.

\paragraph{What a winding number counts.} \textbf{[standard]} A winding number is the integer-valued closed line integral of a phase, $n = (1/2\pi)\oint d\phi$, the same family as the Aharonov--Bohm and Berry phases, and the line integrals that Papers~\#29--\#31~\cite{hanamura29,hanamura30,hanamura31} take as fundamental observables. What it counts is fixed by the path. \textbf{[0SM reading]} Round the origin of the amplitude plane it counts the sign of charge, $\pm1$, and it does not change whether the electron is bound or free. Round a closed orbit about a nucleus it counts the energy level. The spacing of orbital levels shrinks with the size of the path: $10.2$~eV between the first two levels of hydrogen, but $8.6\times10^{-10}$~eV for an electron circulating in a $1436$~m storage ring at $208.8$~kHz, so that the radiation from such a ring looks continuous while the charge winding never changes. The assignment of the internal layers $\tfrac12$, $1$, $2$ to spin, charge and quadrupole is open.

\subsection{The Quartic Energy as a Quadratic Form}
\label{subsec:g_hopf}

\paragraph{Motivation.} In quantum mechanics the expected energy is a quadratic form in the state, $\expval{H} = \psi^{\dagger}H\psi$. The kernel energies $T_A = E_0a^{4}$ and $T_B = E_0b^{4}$ are of fourth degree in the amplitudes. Paper~\#65 reconciles the two by changing variables rather than by identifying the quartic with a quadratic form directly.

\textbf{[standard]} Collect the amplitudes into a two-component vector, the kernel rotor,
\begin{equation}
\chi \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} \cos(\iphase/2) \\ \sin(\iphase/2) \end{pmatrix},
\qquad \chi^{\dagger}\chi = 1,
\label{eq:g_rotor}
\end{equation}
and form the three-component Bloch vector with the Pauli matrices,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{s} \;=\; \chi^{\dagger}\bm{\sigma}\chi \;=\; (\sin\iphase,\ 0,\ \cos\iphase).
\label{eq:g_bloch}
\end{equation}
The rotor turns through $\iphase/2$ while the Bloch vector turns through $\iphase$: the Bloch vector moves at twice the angle. After $\iphase$ advances by $2\pi$ the Bloch vector is back, but the rotor has changed sign, $\chi(\iphase + 2\pi) = -\chi(\iphase)$; it returns only after $4\pi$. The Bloch vector is built from $\chi$ quadratically and cannot tell $\chi$ from $-\chi$, so two rotor states correspond to one Bloch state. This is the double cover, the map $\mathrm{SU}(2)\to\mathrm{SO}(3)$ realised here by the Hopf map. Figure~\ref{fig:g_cover} draws it.

\textbf{[0SM reading]} With $n_A = a^{2} = (1 + s_z)/2$ and $n_B = b^{2} = (1 - s_z)/2$, the kernel energies become
\begin{equation}
T_A \;=\; \frac{E_0}{4}\,(1 + s_z)^{2}, \qquad T_B \;=\; \frac{E_0}{4}\,(1 - s_z)^{2},
\label{eq:g_TAs}
\end{equation}
which are quadratic in the Bloch component $s_z$. Quartic in the rotor, quadratic in the Bloch vector: the two descriptions are one structure seen through the double cover. Paper~\#65 shows further that these forms follow uniquely from the radiation-gradient relation of Paper~\#1, the oscillator kinetic energy and energy conservation, so the fourth power is no longer an independent assumption. The Stefan--Boltzmann motivation of Paper~\#1 survives as a heuristic, and its literal form, a kernel temperature proportional to $\cos(\iphase/2)$, is withdrawn because such a temperature would turn negative for $\iphase > \pi$ and change sign under $\chi\to-\chi$. Temperature belongs to the occupation layer. The residual assumption is now the radiation-gradient relation itself. The standard spinor of spin~$\tfrac12$ also changes sign under a $2\pi$ rotation and returns after $4\pi$; that the internal rotor and the spatial spinor are physically the same object is not shown.

\subsection{The Dirac Equation and the Two Branches}
\label{subsec:g_dirac}

\textbf{[standard]} For a free particle $E^{2} = p^{2}c^{2} + m^{2}c^{4}$, so at rest $E = \pm mc^{2}$, and the Dirac equation has four independent rest solutions, two energy signs times two spin states. A state that mixes the two energy signs shows a rapid oscillation of the position, Zitterbewegung, which the Foldy--Wouthuysen transformation removes from states built of positive energy alone.

\textbf{[0SM reading]} Paper~\#65 proves that the rotor obeys the first-order equation $i\,\partial_t\chi = (\omega/2)\sigma_y\chi$ and is unitarily equivalent to the Dirac rest solutions (its Theorem~VI.1). The occupation of a kernel then splits into a part from each branch alone and a cross term between them, $n_A = \tfrac14 + \tfrac14 + \tfrac12\cos\iphase$: the exchange between the kernels is the interference of the positive- and negative-energy branches. The negative branch is read as the absorbing side of a paired emission and absorption: one kernel emits, the other absorbs, and the roles alternate. This is an internal reading of the sign; it does not deny antiparticles or replace their field-theoretic description. The complex unit $i$ of the equation is not imported: the position--momentum pair of a harmonic oscillator turns in phase space, and $i$ is the generator of that turning.

\paragraph{The exchange drawn as a Riemann surface.} \textbf{[standard]} The square root is two-valued; continuing it once round a branch point moves it to the other sheet. \textbf{[0SM reading]} The flow of energy is a function of this kind, $\dot n_A = \mp\omega\sqrt{n_An_B}$. On one sheet $A$ radiates and $B$ absorbs; on the other $B$ radiates and $A$ absorbs. The branch points are the two kernel states $\{0,1\} = S^{0}$, where the flow stops and reverses, and the cut is the segment $D^{1}$. The Dirac energy $E(p) = \sqrt{p^{2}c^{2} + m^{2}c^{4}}$ has the same two-sheeted structure, with branch points at $p = \pm imc$.

\paragraph{Charge and spin.} \textbf{[0SM reading]} The order in which the exchange runs over the two sheets is the sign of $ab$, which is the winding: it is charge, not spin. Two rotors turning in opposite senses, $H_4 = \mathrm{diag}(H,-H)$, have the form of the Dirac rest polynomial, but their degeneracy is between the two senses of rotation, so the reversed rotor is the charge partner (electron and positron). Spin is assigned instead to the spatial direction in which radiation and absorption progress along the helical path of Paper~\#51, a direction the rotor does not carry. The four Dirac rest solutions are then read as \{radiating or absorbing role\} $\times$ \{spin\}. How the spin candidate responds to spatial rotations, and the projection law $\cos^{2}(\theta/2)$ in a Stern--Gerlach field, are open.

\subsection{The Internal Clock}
\label{subsec:g_clock}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} Paper~\#65 separates two rates inside the electron (Table~\ref{tab:g_clocks}). The phase of each Dirac branch advances at $mc^{2}/\hbar$, a frequency of $1.2356\times10^{20}$~Hz, the light-speed reference $\nuC$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:nuC}. The transport of energy from one kernel to the other runs at the speed $\betaZB c$, with $\betaZB$ fixed by the measured anomalous moment through
\begin{align}

\subsection{Two Kernels and the Transport Cycle}
\label{subsec:primer_cycle}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} The 0-Sphere model replaces the point electron by two kernels, $A$ and $B$, separated by the Compton wavelength $\lambdaC = h/mc$~\cite{hanamura01}. The electron's rest energy is not stored at either kernel permanently. It is carried from one kernel to the other and back: while one kernel radiates, the other absorbs the same energy, so that the occupations obey $n_A + n_B = 1$ at every instant~\cite{hanamura65}. Paper~\#10~\cite{hanamura10} fixes the speed of this transport. The energy moves from one kernel to the other at the average speed
\begin{equation}
\vZB \;=\; \betaZB\,c, \qquad \betaZB = 0.040472,
\label{eq:vZB}
\end{equation}
about $12{,}133$~km/s, and the number of transports per second is the distance divided into the speed,
\begin{equation}
\nuZB \;=\; \frac{\betaZB\,c}{\lambdaC} \;=\; 5.0007\times10^{18}\ \text{Hz}.
\label{eq:nuZB}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:nuZB} is the frequency convention of the whole paper. One transport, from one kernel to the other, lasts $\Ttr = 1/\nuZB = 1.9997\times10^{-19}$~s. Paper~\#65 reads this tick as the internal time of the electron, so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:nuZB} can be read the other way round: the kernel separation is the transport speed times one tick,
\begin{equation}
\lambdaC \;=\; \betaZB\,c\,\Ttr .
\label{eq:clock}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The Bridge Equation: Where $\betaZB$ Comes From}
\label{subsec:primer_bridge}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} The value $\betaZB = 0.040472$ is not fitted. Paper~\#10 identifies the anomalous magnetic moment with the fractional Lorentz contraction of the internal transport path, $\lvert a\rvert = (L_0 - L)/L_0$, so that the mass-determining Lorentz factor is $\gamma_L = 1 + \lvert a\rvert$. For the velocity the series uses the root-mean-square placement of Paper~\#62~\cite{hanamura62}: the momentum-carrying term of the energy identity has a mean-to-peak ratio whose square root is exactly $1/\sqrt{2}$, which gives
\begin{equation}
\gv \;=\; 1 + \frac{\lvert a\rvert}{\sqrt{2}},
\qquad
\betaZB \;=\; \sqrt{1 - \gv^{-2}} .
\label{eq:bridge}
\end{equation}
For the electron, $a_e = 1.15965218059\times10^{-3}$~\cite{fan2023} gives $\betaZB^{2} = 0.0016379809$ and $\betaZB = 0.040472$, the canonical value of the series, used by Papers~\#51, \#61, \#62 and~\#65 and throughout the present paper. The modulus in Eq.~\eqref{eq:bridge} is chosen so that the equation also applies to particles whose moment is negative~\cite{hanamura62}.

The property of $\betaZB$ on which both tests turn is visible in Eq.~\eqref{eq:bridge}: \emph{$\betaZB$ is built from a dimensionless measured input and is a pure number.} It does not depend on mass, on position, on binding or on the state of motion. Section~\ref{sec:bound} shows that the model passes the binding test because of this. Section~\ref{sec:composition} shows that it fails the composition test because the pure number is a different number for different species.

\subsection{The Thomas Vortex}
\label{subsec:primer_omt}

\textbf{[standard]} Successive Lorentz boosts along non-parallel directions do not commute, so a body whose velocity $\vb{v}$ and acceleration $\vb{a}$ are not parallel precesses at the Thomas rate $\bm{\Omega} = (\gamma - 1)(\vb{v}\times\vb{a})/v^{2}$~\cite{Thomas1926,Jackson1999}. \textbf{[0SM reading]} The transport path between the kernels is curved, so the transported energy precesses throughout each transport; Paper~\#19~\cite{hanamura19} established this as a real internal angular velocity. With $\vb{v} \propto \cos\iphase\,\ehat{x}$ along the kernel axis and $\vb{a} \propto -\sin\iphase\,\ehat{y}$ across it, Paper~\#58 obtains
\begin{equation}
\OmT(\iphase) \;=\; -\,\frac{\vZB^{2}\,\omZB}{4c^{2}}\,\sin 2\iphase\;\ehat{z}, \qquad \iphase = \omZB t .
\label{eq:OmT}
\end{equation}
The factor $\sin 2\iphase$ has half the period of the transport cycle. Paper~\#48~\cite{hanamura48} reads the three periodicities $4\pi$, $2\pi$ and $\pi$ present in the cycle as spin $\tfrac12$, $1$ and $2$, which makes the $\pi$-periodic $\OmT$ the series' candidate for the spin-2, gravitational sector. The identification is a candidate: the demonstration that the $\pi$-periodic structure carries rank-2 tensor transformation properties has been open since Paper~\#48, and Paper~\#65 records the correspondence of the layers $\tfrac12$, $1$, $2$ with spin, charge and quadrupole as open. Section~\ref{subsec:rank2} returns to it.

Two features of Eq.~\eqref{eq:OmT} are used below and both are robust. Its amplitude is proportional to $\vZB^{2}\omZB$, and its integral over one period $2\pi/\omZB$ is $\vZB^{2}/c^{2}$, independent of $\omZB$. Paper~\#58 writes $\omZB = 2\pi\nuZB$; how many transports one period of $\iphase$ contains is the convention question of entry C3 of Sec.~\ref{sec:corrections}, and it changes the amplitude of $\OmT$ by a fixed factor without changing either feature.

\subsection{How the Series Proposes That Gravity Is Generated}
\label{subsec:primer_gravity}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} The mechanism has three stages~\cite{hanamura34,hanamura49,hanamura58}. In the first, the Thomas precession builds an outward stress on the transported energy, and when the stress reaches the binding floor $E_0/2$ a fragment of energy is ejected; the $\sin 2\iphase$ structure gives two ejections per cycle, forward and backward, so that the pair carries no net momentum but a symmetric polarisation. In the second, a neighbouring system absorbs the fragment, its internal phase shifts, and the synchronisation energy turns into a translational drift that appears as free fall. In the third, the weakness of gravity is attributed to a small probability, a per-cycle suppression written $\epsilon_2\epsilon_3 \sim 10^{-5}$, which is at present a parametrisation and not a derived quantity. Newton's constant and the ratio $F_{\text{grav}}/F_{\text{EM}} \sim 10^{-42}$ have not been derived, and there is no field theory with a propagator. The framework offers a mechanism, and the tests of this paper examine whether the charge that the mechanism assigns is the one the equivalence principle requires.

\subsection{Paper~\#52's Derivation of the First Clause}
\label{subsec:primer_wep}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} In a field $\vb{g} = -g\hat{g}$ the two kernels sit at different gravitational potentials, separated along $\hat{g}$ by $\lambdaC(\hat{e}\cdot\hat{g})$, where $\hat{e}$ is the kernel axis (Fig.~\ref{fig:cancel}). The energy carried across that separation in one tick acquires the asymmetry
\begin{equation}
\delta E \;=\; m\,g\,\lambdaC\,(\hat{e}\cdot\hat{g}),
\qquad
\delta p \;=\; \frac{\delta E}{c},
\label{eq:dE52}
\end{equation}
and the force is the momentum transferred per tick times the number of ticks per second:
\begin{align}
F
&\;=\; \delta p \times \nuZB
\;=\; \frac{m\,g\,\lambdaC}{c}\times\frac{\betaZB\,c}{\lambdaC}
\notag\\
&\;=\; m\,g\,\betaZB\,(\hat{e}\cdot\hat{g}) .
\label{eq:F52}
\end{align}
The Compton wavelength cancels between the lever arm and the rate, so the force is proportional to $m$ for any $m$. Both appearances of $\lambdaC$ belong to the same object, the kernel axis, which is why Paper~\#52 calls the cancellation structural rather than algebraic. What remains is the factor $\betaZB$. With Eq.~\eqref{eq:nuC}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:F52} can also be written
\begin{equation}
F \;=\; m\,g\,(\hat{e}\cdot\hat{g})\;\frac{\nuZB}{\nuC},
\label{eq:F52ratio}
\end{equation}
which is the form used in Sec.~\ref{subsec:constraint}: one transfer of $\delta p$ per transport tick gives $mg\,\betaZB$, one transfer per Compton cycle would give $mg$. Paper~\#52 recorded the recovery of $F = mg$ as an open task.

\subsection{What Relativity Says a Bound Electron Weighs}
\label{subsec:primer_bound}

\textbf{[standard]} The gravitational mass of a composite system is its total energy divided by $c^{2}$, binding included. For a hydrogen-like ion of nuclear charge $Z$ the exact Dirac energy of the $1s$ state, for a point nucleus, is
\begin{equation}
E_{1s} \;=\; mc^{2}\,\gammaD, \qquad \gammaD \;=\; \sqrt{1 - (Z\alpha)^{2}},
\label{eq:EDirac}
\end{equation}
so the binding energy is $B = mc^{2}(1-\gammaD)$ and the electron contributes $\gammaD\,m$ to the mass of the ion. In hydrogen the defect is $2.7\times10^{-5}$ of the electron mass. In hydrogen-like uranium, $Z\alpha = 0.671$, $\gammaD = 0.7411$, and the electron contributes $74\%$ of its rest mass. A $26\%$ effect cannot be confused with a correction, which makes the heavy hydrogen-like ions the right test bench. For $Z = 92$ the finite nuclear size reduces $B$ from $132.28$ to $132.08$~keV and QED reduces it to about $131.8$~keV, consistent with the measured ground-state Lamb shift~\cite{gumberidze2005}; these are tenths of a percent against a test at $26\%$, and since the principle concerns the measured total energy, corrections contained in that energy appear on both sides of the comparison.

\subsection{What an E\"otv\"os Experiment Measures}
\label{subsec:primer_eotvos}

\textbf{[standard]} Two bodies of different material fall in the same field and their accelerations are compared through
\begin{equation}
\eta(A,B) \;=\; 2\,\frac{a_A - a_B}{a_A + a_B}
\;=\; \left(\frac{m_g}{m_i}\right)_{\!A} - \left(\frac{m_g}{m_i}\right)_{\!B}.
\label{eq:eta}
\end{equation}
The MICROSCOPE satellite, comparing titanium and platinum alloy test masses in orbit, reported $\eta(\mathrm{Ti},\mathrm{Pt}) = [-1.5 \pm 2.3(\text{stat}) \pm 1.5(\text{syst})]\times10^{-15}$, a sensitivity of $2.7\times10^{-15}$~\cite{microscope2022}; torsion balances reach $\eta(\mathrm{Be},\mathrm{Ti}) = (0.3\pm1.8)\times10^{-13}$ and $\eta(\mathrm{Be},\mathrm{Al}) = (-0.7\pm1.3)\times10^{-13}$~\cite{schlamminger2008,wagner2012}. Such an experiment tests \emph{how} each form of energy gravitates, because materials store their mass in different proportions (Fig.~\ref{fig:budget}). If one form gravitated with a different strength per unit energy, $\eta$ would show the difference multiplied by the difference in that form's mass fraction. Both tests below use this lever.

\section{Test I: The Cancellation for a Bound Electron}
\label{sec:bound}
% ========================================

\paragraph{Motivation.} The cancellation of Paper~\#52 was carried out for a free electron, whose energy is its rest energy. A bound electron has less energy, $E = \gammaD mc^{2}$, and the equivalence principle requires it to gravitate by exactly that much less. The test is whether the internal cycle, sized and timed by the electron's energy, delivers the reduction without adjustment.

\subsection{The Internal Scale of a Bound Electron}
\label{subsec:lambda_eff}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} The kernel separation $\lambdaC = h/mc$ is the Compton wavelength of the rest energy. For a bound electron of total energy $E$ the corresponding length is
\begin{equation}
\lambdaC^{\text{eff}} \;=\; \frac{hc}{E} \;=\; \frac{\lambdaC}{\gammaD}.
\label{eq:lameff}
\end{equation}
This is the one structural assumption the present paper adds to the series. It states that the internal scale tracks the total energy rather than the rest energy, which is the natural reading of a Compton length, and it is not derived elsewhere in the corpus. A reader who rejects it should read this section as a conditional. In the clock language of Eq.~\eqref{eq:clock} the assumption says that binding lengthens the tick, $\Ttr \to \Ttr/\gammaD$, while the transport speed stays at $\betaZB c$, because $\betaZB$ is a pure number fixed by the anomalous moment. The transport frequency then falls in the same proportion as the energy,
\begin{equation}
\nuZB^{\text{bound}} \;=\; \frac{\betaZB c}{\lambdaC^{\text{eff}}} \;=\; \frac{\betaZB\,E}{h} \;=\; \gammaD\,\nuZB \;\propto\; E .
\label{eq:nuE}
\end{equation}
The ruler lengthens, the clock slows, the speed is untouched (Fig.~\ref{fig:ruler}).

\subsection{Redoing the Cancellation}
\label{subsec:redo}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} The two ingredients of Eq.~\eqref{eq:F52} become, for a bound electron,
\begin{align}
\delta E &\;=\; \frac{E}{c^{2}}\,g\,\lambdaC^{\text{eff}}\,(\hat{e}\cdot\hat{g}),
\qquad
\delta p \;=\; \frac{\delta E}{c},
\label{eq:dE_bound}\\[2pt]
\text{ticks per second} &\;=\; \nuZB^{\text{bound}} \;=\; \frac{\betaZB c}{\lambdaC^{\text{eff}}} .
\label{eq:rate_bound}
\end{align}
Their product again loses the internal scale:
\begin{equation}
F \;=\; \delta p\times\nuZB^{\text{bound}}
\;=\; \frac{E}{c^{2}}\,g\,\betaZB\,(\hat{e}\cdot\hat{g}) .
\label{eq:F_bound}
\end{equation}
The mass factor is $E/c^{2}$, which is the gravitational mass the equivalence principle assigns to the bound electron. The binding-energy defect is carried with no adjustment. The reason is the same one that made Paper~\#52's cancellation work: $\lambdaC^{\text{eff}}$ enters the lever arm in the numerator and the rate in the denominator, so any change in the internal scale, whether caused by a change of mass or by binding, leaves the force untouched except through the energy that both roles share.

\subsection{The Same Result from the Thomas Vortex}
\label{subsec:OmT_reduce}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} An independent route gives the same answer. With $\omZB = 2\pi\nuZB^{\text{bound}}$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:nuE}, the amplitude of Eq.~\eqref{eq:OmT} becomes
\begin{equation}
\lvert\OmT\rvert_{\max}
\;=\; \frac{\vZB^{2}}{4c^{2}}\cdot\frac{\vZB\,E}{\hbar c}
\;=\; \frac{\vZB^{3}}{4\hbar c^{3}}\;E ,
\label{eq:OmT_E}
\end{equation}
with $\vZB^{3}/4\hbar c^{3} = 1.5715\times10^{29}$~J$^{-1}$\,s$^{-1}$ a constant. The Thomas vortex is strictly linear in the total energy. It tracks $E$ and not $m$ because $\vZB$ is anchored to $a_e$ and does not respond to binding. Had $\vZB$ been allowed to scale with the energy, Eq.~\eqref{eq:OmT_E} would have acquired a higher power of $E$ and the test would have failed; Sec.~\ref{subsec:nuclear_lever} shows by how much.

This locates the reason the model passes the test. The equivalence principle is satisfied here by the bridge equation, which belongs to the anomalous-moment sector of the series. Papers~\#10 and~\#62, written about $g-2$, are what make Papers~\#52 and~\#58 compatible with the binding clause.

\section{Two Exact Results About Dirac Bound States}
\label{sec:theorems}
% ========================================

\paragraph{Motivation.} The binding test rests on the total energy of a Dirac--Coulomb state. Two exact statements about such states are available, and the second concerns the model directly: in a bound state, the negative-energy content and the binding-energy defect are the same quantity.

\subsection{The Statements}
\label{subsec:theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Expectation of $\beta$ in a Coulomb field]
\label{thm:beta}
\textbf{[standard]} Let $H = c\,\bm{\alpha}\cdot\vb{p} + \beta mc^{2} - Z\alpha\hbar c/r$ and let $\psi$ be a bound eigenstate with eigenvalue $E$. Then
\begin{equation}
\expval{\beta} \;=\; \frac{E}{mc^{2}} .
\label{eq:thm}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Hellmann--Feynman theorem~\cite{feynman1939} applied to the parameter $m$ gives $\partial E/\partial m = c^{2}\expval{\beta}$. The Coulomb coupling $Z\alpha$ is dimensionless, so the only energy scale of the problem is $mc^{2}$ and $E = mc^{2}\,\mathcal{F}(Z\alpha;n,\kappa)$. Hence $\partial E/\partial m = E/m$, and the two expressions give Eq.~\eqref{eq:thm}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Negative-energy admixture]
\label{cor:admix}
\textbf{[standard]} With large and small radial components $g(r)$, $f(r)$ normalised by $\int_0^\infty (g^{2}+f^{2})\,\dd r = 1$, and $\expval{\beta} = \int_0^\infty (g^{2}-f^{2})\,\dd r$,
\begin{equation}
\int_0^\infty f^{2}\,\dd r \;=\; \frac{1}{2}\left(1 - \frac{E}{mc^{2}}\right) \;=\; \frac{B}{2mc^{2}} .
\label{eq:cor}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

Neither statement is new. Hellmann--Feynman and virial methods give the expectation values of the Dirac matrices in the Coulomb problem systematically~\cite{adkins2008}. The derivation is given because the result is used below, because its one condition, scale-freeness of the potential, is what fails for a finite nucleus, and because the reading that follows concerns the model.

\subsection{What the Corollary Says Inside the Model}
\label{subsec:why_theorem}

\textbf{[standard]} The small component of a bound Dirac spinor measures its admixture of negative-energy content, and the interference between positive- and negative-energy amplitudes is the origin of Zitterbewegung. Corollary~\ref{cor:admix} therefore states that the Zitterbewegung content of a Coulomb bound state equals half its fractional binding energy, exactly and for every state: $0.1294$ for the $1s$ electron of uranium, $1.33\times10^{-5}$ for hydrogen.

\textbf{[0SM reading]} In a framework where the transport between the kernels carries the gravitational coupling, this is a consistency signal. The quantity the model uses to generate gravity and the quantity relativity says must be subtracted from the gravitating mass are, in the Coulomb case, tied by an identity. Two distinctions keep the reading honest. First, the corollary is a theorem of standard Dirac theory, and what belongs to the model is only its interpretation. Second, the admixture of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cor} is a property of the bound-state spinor and vanishes for a free electron at rest, whereas the interior described in Paper~\#65 is an equal superposition of the two branches for every electron, free or bound, with the cross term as the exchange. How the two are related is not settled here.

\subsection{Where the Statement Stops, and the Numbers}
\label{subsec:theorem_boundary}

The proof used scale-freeness, so a nucleus of finite size should break the identity, and it does. For $Z = 92$ with a uniformly charged sphere of radius $R = 7.561$~fm, the uniform-sphere equivalent of the measured charge radius, direct integration gives $E/mc^{2} = 0.7415241$ but $\expval{\beta} = 0.7420919$, and $\int f^{2} = 0.128954$ against $B/2mc^{2} = 0.129238$. The identity is broken by $0.22\%$, of the order of the nuclear radius in units of the $1s$ orbit. The identity is exact where the Coulomb law is exact and fails smoothly where it is not, which also shows that the numerics do not produce it by construction.

Table~\ref{tab:theorem} verifies both statements for seven states at three nuclear charges by two-sided shooting on the radial Dirac equations (Appendix~\ref{app:methods}). The energies are located by the shooting itself, not taken from the closed form, and the expectation values are accumulated inside the integrator. Figure~\ref{fig:admix} shows the admixture against $Z$.

\section{The Internal Cycle Under Binding}
\label{sec:cycle}
% ========================================

Binding changes the summary quantities by single powers of $\gammaD$, and it is useful to see what it does to the cycle itself. Figure~\ref{fig:omt} shows $\OmT(t)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:OmT} for a nearly free electron and for the $1s$ electron of uranium. The two curves have the same shape. Binding lowers the amplitude by $\gammaD = 0.7411$ and lengthens the period by $1/\gammaD = 1.3493$, so the area under one period, the Thomas angle accumulated per cycle, is unchanged:
\begin{equation}
\oint \lvert\OmT\rvert\,\dd t \;=\; \frac{\vZB^{2}}{c^{2}} \;=\; 1.637981\times10^{-3}\ \text{rad},
\label{eq:invariant}
\end{equation}
independent of $Z$ and of the convention for $\omZB$. The internal geometry is untouched by binding; only the rate at which it is traversed changes. This is the behaviour the equivalence principle requires of any clock in a potential well.

\subsection{The Consequence for Bulk Matter}
\label{subsec:bulk}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} Read species by species, Eq.~\eqref{eq:F52} makes a body of electrons and nucleons feel a force proportional to $\sum_s m_s\betaZB^{s}$ rather than to $\sum_s m_s$. Normalised to the nucleon value, the ratio of gravitational to inertial mass is
\begin{equation}
\frac{m_g}{m_i} \;=\; x_e\,\frac{\betaZB^{e}}{\betaZB^{p}} + (1 - x_e),
\label{eq:mgmi}
\end{equation}
with $x_e$ the electron mass fraction. Since $\betaZB^{e}/\betaZB^{p} = 0.0451$, electrons pull with $4.5\%$ of the strength their mass suggests, and a material with more electrons per unit mass falls more slowly. The electron fraction is $2.521\times10^{-4}$ for titanium and $2.193\times10^{-4}$ for platinum, which has the larger neutron excess. Equation~\eqref{eq:mgmi} then gives
\begin{equation}
\eta(\mathrm{Ti},\mathrm{Pt}) \;=\; 3.13\times10^{-5},
\label{eq:eta_pred}
\end{equation}
against the MICROSCOPE sensitivity of $2.7\times10^{-15}$. The prediction exceeds the bound by a factor of $1.2\times10^{10}$. Figure~\ref{fig:eta} shows this and three further material pairs.

\subsection{The Nuclear-Binding Lever}
\label{subsec:nuclear_lever}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} A second and independent lever constrains a different family of readings. If the gravitational charge were not linear in energy, for instance if it were identified with the rate of fragment emission times the energy per fragment, which scales as $E^{2}$, then the mass defect would count twice at leading order. Nuclear binding is about $0.9\%$ of the mass of ordinary matter and varies between nuclides (Fig.~\ref{fig:budget}), so the doubling would appear directly in $\eta$. The binding fraction is $9.375\times10^{-3}$ for $^{48}$Ti and $8.511\times10^{-3}$ for $^{195}$Pt, so a charge quadratic in energy gives $\eta(\mathrm{Ti},\mathrm{Pt}) = 8.64\times10^{-4}$, $3.2\times10^{11}$ times the bound, and $1.20\times10^{-3}$ for beryllium against uranium. The same reading fails more directly on the hydrogen atom: doubling its $13.6$~eV defect shifts $m(^{1}\mathrm{H})$ by $1.46\times10^{-8}$~u, against a measured uncertainty of $1.4\times10^{-11}$~u~\cite{ame2020}.

Figure~\ref{fig:fork} gives the bound-state form of the same statement: the gravitating mass of a $1s$ electron for charges scaling as $E$, $E^{2}$ and $E^{3}$. Only the first follows the equivalence principle. At $Z = 92$ the second is short by $98.0$~keV and the third by $170.7$~keV.

\section{What Survives, and What It Asks of the Completing Mechanism}
\label{sec:survives}
% ========================================

\subsection{A Division of Labour}
\label{subsec:division}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} The two tests point to one reorganisation. Paper~\#58's argument for $\OmT$ is that its $\pi$-periodicity supplies the rank-2 \emph{polarisation} of the mediator, an argument about what kind of field gravity is and not about how strongly it couples. Nothing in that argument requires $\OmT$ to set the coupling strength as well, and the results above say that it must not:
\begin{align}
\underbrace{\OmT}_{\text{$\pi$-periodic}}
&\;\longrightarrow\;
\underbrace{\text{polarisation of the mediator}}_{\text{what kind of field}}
\notag\\
\underbrace{E}_{\text{total energy}}
&\;\longrightarrow\;
\underbrace{\text{strength of the coupling}}_{\text{gravitational charge}} .
\label{eq:division}
\end{align}
Read this way, the composition test removes nothing the series has established. It removes an identification the series had left implicit and replaces it with a sharper one.

\subsection{The Requirement on Paper~\#52's Open Task}
\label{subsec:constraint}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} Paper~\#52 recorded the recovery of $F = mg$ from $F = mg\,\betaZB$ as an open task, and such a task reads naturally as the search for a missing factor of about $25$. Table~\ref{tab:species} shows that this reading is not available: the factor is $24.71$ for an electron, $24.64$ for a muon, $1.114$ for a proton and $1.105$ for a neutron. The frequency form Eq.~\eqref{eq:F52ratio} says what the factor is. The force counts one transfer $\delta p$ per transport tick, at $\nuZB$; Newtonian free fall would need one transfer per cycle of the light-speed reference, at $\nuC$; and the ratio $\nuC/\nuZB = 1/\betaZB$ is different for every species, because each species' transport clock runs at its own fraction of its own reference.

\paragraph{Completion requirement.} \emph{Any mechanism completing the derivation of Paper~\#52 must supply a factor exactly equal to $1/\betaZB = \nuC/\nuZB$ for each species, so that the transport speed cancels from the gravitational force identically. Equivalently, the number of transfers per second and the momentum per transfer must combine so that only the total energy survives: either the transfers are counted at the light-speed reference rather than at the transport rate, or each transport delivers $\delta E/\vZB$ rather than $\delta E/c$.}

The two equivalent forms are statements about the product, not proposals for a mechanism. The requirement is constructive and can be checked inside the model. It says where to look: at whatever governs the probability that a transport results in an exchange. If that probability carried $1/\betaZB$, so that slower transport were compensated by more efficient exchange, the cancellation would be structural in the same sense as Paper~\#52's cancellation of $\lambdaC$.

\subsection{The Obvious Home for the Factor, and Why a First Pass Discourages It}
\label{subsec:epsilon}

The suppression factor $\epsilon_2\epsilon_3 \sim 10^{-5}$ of Papers~\#34 and~\#49 is the natural place for such a dependence, and this is the first quantitative demand the series has placed on it. A first pass does not encourage it. In Paper~\#49 the ejection is triggered when the Thomas-precession stress reaches the binding floor, and that stress scales as $\vZB^{2}$. A nucleon, with $\betaZB = 0.898$, would carry some five hundred times the stress of an electron and would eject more readily, not less. The dependence runs as $\betaZB^{2}$ where $\betaZB^{-1}$ is needed. The escape is that $\epsilon_2\epsilon_3$ may belong to the absorption side: Paper~\#34 introduces it within the synchronisation picture as a suppression of the coupling between neighbours rather than as a property of the emission. Settling which requires a close reading of Papers~\#34 and~\#49, and is the immediate next check.

\subsection{A Second, Weaker Handle}
\label{subsec:muon}

If the composition problem were resolved without cancelling $\betaZB$, a residual prediction would remain inside the lepton family. Because $a_\mu$ and $a_e$ differ by $0.54\%$, readings that keep $\betaZB$ make a muon and an electron gravitate differently per unit mass by $0.27\%$. No experiment approaches this sensitivity for muons. It is recorded because it is the one place where the species dependence appears within a single family and is therefore free of the composite-structure caveat of Sec.~\ref{subsec:composite}.

\established{The bridge equation gives leptons and nucleons transport speeds a factor $22$ apart, so the force law of Paper~\#52 predicts $\eta(\mathrm{Ti},\mathrm{Pt}) = 3.13\times10^{-5}$, ten orders of magnitude above the bound. A charge non-linear in energy fails through nuclear binding. The missing factor $1/\betaZB = \nuC/\nuZB$ differs between species, so it must cancel the transport speed rather than rescale the force.}

\section{An Incompatibility Between Papers~\#62 and~\#63, and Its Resolution}
\label{sec:tension}
% ========================================

\paragraph{Motivation.} Test~I turned on $\vZB$ being a pure number. One paper of the series says it is; another needs it not to be. The two statements cannot both be local, and measurement decides between them.

\subsection{The Two Statements and the Two Readings}
\label{subsec:two_statements}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} Paper~\#62 argues that the anomalous moment is a single frame-independent invariant and is explicit about its independence of external fields. Since $\betaZB$ is a function of $a$ alone, this makes $\betaZB$ a constant of nature. Paper~\#63, following the redshift of internal clocks of Paper~\#22~\cite{hanamura22}, argues that near a horizon the internal frequency and the speed $\vZB$ are driven to zero, so that $\OmT \propto \vZB^{2}$ vanishes, ejection stops, and the gravitational coupling freezes rather than diverging. If $\betaZB$ is fixed by a field-independent $a$, it cannot go to zero anywhere; if it goes to zero locally, $a$ depends on the gravitational potential.

\paragraph{Reading A: $\vZB$ is a local invariant.} Test~I stands, and the freezing of Paper~\#63 describes quantities inferred by a distant observer. This is consistent, but general relativity already predicts that a distant observer sees internal processes freeze near a horizon, so the freezing is no longer a point of divergence from general relativity.

\paragraph{Reading B: $\vZB$ decreases locally.} Then $a$ depends on the potential $\Phi$. With $\vZB \propto \sqrt{1 + 2\Phi/c^{2}}$, as the redshift argument suggests, and $\betaZB^{2} \approx \sqrt{2}\,\lvert a\rvert$ at small $a$, one obtains $a \propto 1 + 2\Phi/c^{2}$ at leading order, that is
\begin{equation}
k_{a} \;\equiv\; \frac{c^{2}}{a}\,\dv{a}{\Phi} \;=\; 2 .
\label{eq:ka}
\end{equation}
This is a violation of local position invariance with a coefficient of order unity, and local position invariance is measured.

\subsection{Reading B Is Excluded by Clock Comparisons}
\label{subsec:LPI}

\textbf{[standard]} Clock comparisons bound exactly this kind of coefficient for the fundamental constants, using the annual change of the solar potential at the Earth. Monitoring nearly degenerate levels of dysprosium gave $k_\alpha = (-8.7\pm6.6)\times10^{-6}$ for the fine-structure constant~\cite{ferrell2007}. Comparing two optical transitions of $^{171}$Yb$^{+}$ with caesium fountains over several years improved this to~\cite{lange2021}
\begin{equation}
k_\alpha \;=\; \frac{c^{2}}{\alpha}\dv{\alpha}{\Phi} \;=\; 14(11)\times10^{-9} .
\label{eq:kalpha}
\end{equation}
In quantum electrodynamics $a_e = \alpha/2\pi - 0.328(\alpha/\pi)^{2} + \dots$, so $k_a = k_\alpha$ at leading order. Reading~B requires $2$ where measurement allows at most $2.5\times10^{-8}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:lpi}). \emph{Reading~B is excluded by eight orders of magnitude}, and was already excluded by five orders in 2007.

One loophole should be stated. The bound constrains $\alpha$, and the series takes $a_e$ as measured input rather than deriving it from $\alpha$, so one could hold that $a_e$ varies with $\Phi$ while $\alpha$ does not. The position requires the ten-digit agreement between the measured $a_e$ and the QED series in $\alpha$, which holds at our own potential, to be a coincidence that dissolves elsewhere. It has one remaining test. In a Penning trap $a_e$ is measured as a ratio of two frequencies, so a common redshift of the apparatus cancels, and any annual dependence would be a real violation. With $\Delta\Phi/c^{2} = 3.299\times10^{-10}$ over the year, Reading~B predicts an annual modulation $\Delta(g/2) = a_e k_a\Delta\Phi/c^{2} = 7.65\times10^{-13}$, against a single-measurement uncertainty of $1.3\times10^{-13}$~\cite{fan2023}. A campaign spread over the year, with control of seasonal systematics, would be needed; the comparison is recorded as the residual test of a narrow hypothesis.

\subsection{Consequence for the Series}
\label{subsec:tension_consequence}

Reading~A is forced, and three things follow. First, Test~I stands without qualification: $\vZB$ is a local invariant, so $\OmT \propto E$ is not threatened from this direction. This is also the reading on which Paper~\#65 builds its internal time: the transport clock advances in proportion to proper time, is redshifted as an atomic clock is, and, because $\betaZB$ is a pure number, passes tests of local position invariance automatically. Second, the horizon freezing of Paper~\#63 is a statement about what a distant observer infers. So read, it does not conflict with general relativity and is not a test of the model against it, and the series should not count it as an observational signature. Third, the episode shows a hazard the framework will meet again. Because $\betaZB$ is anchored to a measured constant, any proposal that makes the internal transport respond to the environment becomes a claim that a measured constant varies, and such claims are bounded very tightly. The arithmetic that carries the model through Test~I prevents it from buying environmental responses cheaply.

\subsection{The Rank-2 Question Split in Two}
\label{subsec:rank2}

The requirement that the mediator carry rank-2 tensor structure was stated in Paper~\#48, carried as open task~(vi) of Paper~\#49 and open task~(I) of Paper~\#58, and recorded as open in Paper~\#63. Across those papers it did not move. Part of the reason is that it is two problems with different owners (Fig.~\ref{fig:rank2}).

\paragraph{Problem (a): the source-side tensor structure.} \textbf{[standard]} The mass quadrupole of the proton-trapped electron of Paper~\#59~\cite{hanamura59} is a symmetric rank-2 tensor, and its radiation is computed with the quadrupole formalism of linearised general relativity. The source-side rank-2 structure is therefore available, at the price of importing the radiation formalism of general relativity rather than deriving it. A pair of fragments ejected back to back has invariant mass $2E_{\text{frag}}/c^{2} \neq 0$, so any $J = 2$ configuration of the pair is a massive source and not a massless composite graviton. The Weinberg--Witten theorem~\cite{weinberg1980}, which constrains massless composites, does not reach it, and the Landau--Yang rule~\cite{landau1948,yang1950} forbids only the $J = 1$ channel of two photons. These statements place no obstruction on problem~(a), and they do not by themselves supply the helicity of the pair.

\paragraph{Problem (b): the identification with curvature.} \textbf{[standard]} A spin-2 field requires a symmetric traceless rank-2 tensor, the $(1,1)$ representation of the Lorentz group. The natural curvature object of the series' line-integral programme, the field-strength or Berry-curvature two-form, is antisymmetric and lies in $(1,0)\oplus(0,1)$. No Lorentz-covariant linear map connects the two. A product of two vector objects escapes, since $1\otimes1 = 0\oplus1\oplus2$ has a symmetric traceless part; a single two-form does not. \textbf{[0SM reading]} Problem~(b) is the closure edge of the Berry--Synge--Bonnet--Myers triangle of Paper~\#60~\cite{hanamura60}: the identification of the Berry curvature with the Riemann tensor of the emergent metric, together with open problem O3, the massless radiation from the emergent metric. It is a question about the emergent metric and belongs to the line-integral programme, not to the ejection mechanism.

\subsection{Two Routes to Gravity}
\label{subsec:two_routes}

\textbf{[0SM reading]} The series has pursued two routes to gravity, and everything that fails in this paper belongs to one of them (Fig.~\ref{fig:routes}).

\paragraph{The mediator-construction route.} Papers~\#34, \#49, \#52, \#57, \#58 and~\#59 build gravity from an ejected fragment whose energy, emission rate and polarisation follow from the internal kinematics. On this route the charge is \emph{constructed} from internal quantities. All four rows of Table~\ref{tab:diagnosis} live here, and no available construction succeeds: each is either species-dependent or non-linear in the energy.

\paragraph{The connection-holonomy route.} Papers~\#29, \#30 and~\#31 start elsewhere. Paper~\#31~\cite{hanamura31} takes observables to be holonomies of connections accumulated along histories, with local tensors and curvature as derived descriptors. Paper~\#29~\cite{hanamura29} makes this concrete for the two kernels: the line integral of the spinorial connection along one traversal accumulates a full $2\pi$ of internal phase, so the relevant object is the line integral of a connection rather than the curvature of the path. Paper~\#30~\cite{hanamura30} draws the consequence that matters here: stress--energy is a derived quantity, and the Einstein equation is read as a global consistency condition on accumulated phase. On this route the source is not assembled; it is what the accumulated phase delivers. A phase accumulated along a worldline is an action, and an action carries energy, not transport speed.

The split of Sec.~\ref{subsec:rank2} arrives at the same place from the other direction: with the source-side structure separated off, the remaining burden sits on the closure edge and on O3, both on the holonomy route. Two independent arguments, one from representation theory and one from the equivalence principle, point to the same edge.

\subsection{A Declaration: Restarting the Formalisation from the Line-Integral Series}
\label{subsec:restart}

This paper is recorded as a turning point in the series and not only as an audit. The mediator-construction route has been pushed far enough to show what it cannot do. It cannot supply a gravitational charge, because every internal quantity it offers is tied to the anomalous moment and therefore to the species. It cannot supply a transformation law, because it has no object that transforms. And it has lost its one claimed observational signature (Sec.~\ref{sec:tension}). It keeps a physical picture, and the open tasks of the sector have been formalisation tasks throughout.

The series therefore restarts the formalisation from Papers~\#29, \#30 and~\#31 and carries it forward with the machinery already on that line: the open Wilson line and its four functions~\cite{hanamura55}, the boundary between local-field and directed-path descriptions~\cite{hanamura56}, and the identification of $A_\mu$ that Paper~\#63 names as the bottleneck of the programme. The route has its own difficulty, the representation-theoretic obstruction of Sec.~\ref{subsec:rank2}, and the present paper does not solve it. What it adds is a reason to spend effort there: the alternative route has been shown unable to deliver the charge. This is a programme statement, not a result, and it is not a repudiation of the mediator picture, which may survive as the microscopic realisation of what the holonomy route yields. What the mediator picture cannot be is the definition of the charge.

\subsection{The Composite Nucleon}
\label{subsec:composite}

The most substantial escape from Test~II is that the proton is not an elementary two-kernel system. About $99\%$ of its mass is binding and gluon-field energy of quantum chromodynamics, and Paper~\#61 itself presents the application of the bridge equation to the proton as a structural correspondence. One may therefore hold that $\betaZB^{p}$ is not the quantity that enters Eq.~\eqref{eq:F52} for a nucleon. This is a legitimate position, and it has three costs. The constraint transfers rather than disappears: whatever the constituents carry, the charge of the composite must still come out proportional to its total energy to one part in $10^{15}$, and the model then owes an account of why the constituent speeds cancel. The escape does not touch the lepton family, where the $0.27\%$ muon--electron difference of Sec.~\ref{subsec:muon} survives. And it leaves the open task of Paper~\#52 where Sec.~\ref{subsec:constraint} put it: a factor $24.7$ is missing for the electron, and no universal constant supplies it once a second species is admitted.

\subsection{The Composite Body}
\label{subsec:composite_bulk}

A more radical escape treats a bulk body's internal structure as a property of the whole, so that a titanium atom has one internal cycle with one $\betaZB$ and the species decomposition of Sec.~\ref{subsec:bulk} never arises. The framework does not supply such an object. The two-kernel construction is a construction for an elementary spin-$\tfrac12$ particle, an atom is not a two-kernel system, and there is no anomalous moment that Eq.~\eqref{eq:bridge} could take as input for one. The escape would need new machinery, and if it succeeded it would remove the derivation along with the constraint, since Paper~\#52's argument is made for a single electron in a field.

\subsection{The Assumption Added Here}
\label{subsec:assumption}

Equation~\eqref{eq:lameff}, that the internal scale of a bound electron is $hc/E$, is not established elsewhere in the corpus, and Sec.~\ref{sec:bound} is conditional on it. The conditionality runs in an instructive direction. If the internal scale is held at $h/mc$, the transport frequency does not follow $E$, $\OmT$ does not follow the total energy, and the model fails Test~I. The assumption therefore does not push the result in a favourable direction; it is the minimal condition under which the favourable result is available at all.

\subsection{Other Limits}
\label{subsec:other_limits}

The Dirac calculations use a point nucleus except where stated and omit nuclear recoil, of order $m_e/M \sim 2\times10^{-6}$ for uranium. The E\"otv\"os estimates treat the MICROSCOPE test masses as pure titanium and platinum rather than the alloys flown; alloying changes $x_e$ in the third significant figure and cannot affect a conclusion drawn at ten orders of magnitude. Every number in the text, tables and figures is recomputed by \texttt{verify66.py}, deposited with this paper (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ========================================

Paper~\#52 showed that the 0-Sphere model's gravitational force is proportional to mass because the Compton wavelength enters twice, as lever arm and as inverse rate, and cancels. This paper applied the other two clauses of the equivalence principle, with the internal frequency set throughout at the subluminal transport value of Paper~\#10, $\nuZB = \betaZB c/\lambdaC$, and the two clauses gave opposite answers.

On binding, the model passes exactly. The cancellation for a bound electron returns $F = (E/c^{2})\,g\,\betaZB$, and the Thomas vortex reduces to $\OmT = (\vZB^{3}/4\hbar c^{3})E$; both are linear in the total energy, and the Thomas angle per cycle is the same for every nuclear charge. The reason is that $\betaZB$ is fixed by the anomalous magnetic moment and is a pure number, so the binding clause is underwritten by the $g-2$ sector of the series. In standard Dirac theory, the negative-energy admixture of a Coulomb bound state equals half its fractional binding energy, exactly.

On composition, the model as written does not pass. The bridge equation assigns $\betaZB = 0.0405$ to leptons and $0.898$ to nucleons, and the force law then predicts $\eta(\mathrm{Ti},\mathrm{Pt}) = 3.13\times10^{-5}$, ten orders of magnitude above the MICROSCOPE bound. A charge non-linear in energy fails by a larger margin through nuclear binding. The missing factor of Paper~\#52 has a plain reading: $\betaZB = \nuZB/\nuC$, so the force counts one transfer per transport tick where Newtonian free fall would need one per Compton cycle, and the ratio of the two clocks differs from species to species. The completing mechanism must therefore cancel the transport speed, not rescale the force.

Papers~\#62 and~\#63 make incompatible demands on whether the transport speed is a local invariant, and clock comparisons decide: the local reading needs a coefficient of order unity where measurement allows $2.5\times10^{-8}$. The horizon freezing of Paper~\#63 is a distant-observer statement and not a divergence from general relativity.

The four failures have one cause. A quantity tied by the bridge equation to the anomalous moment, and therefore to the species, has been asked to carry a charge that must be the total energy alone. The property that carries the model through the binding test defeats it in the composition test. The rank-2 question separates into a source-side half, available through the mass quadrupole, and a curvature half that belongs to the emergent metric. The formalisation of the gravitational sector is therefore restarted from the line-integral papers \#29--\#31, where the source is read off from accumulated phase rather than assembled from internal kinematics, under four acceptance criteria. The mediator picture is kept as a picture. It cannot be the definition of the charge.

\section{Numerical Methods}
\label{app:methods}
% ========================================

The radial Dirac equations for $G = rg$ and $F = rf$, in units $\hbar = c = m = 1$, are
\begin{align}
\dv{G}{r} &= -\frac{\kappa}{r}G + \bigl(E - V + 1\bigr)F,
\label{eq:radial1}\\
\dv{F}{r} &= +\frac{\kappa}{r}F - \bigl(E - V - 1\bigr)G,
\label{eq:radial2}
\end{align}
with $V = -Z\alpha/r$ for a point nucleus and $V = -Z\alpha(3 - r^{2}/R^{2})/2R$ inside a uniformly charged sphere of radius $R$. Bound states are located by two-sided shooting. The outward solution starts at $r_0 = 10^{-7}/\lambda$, $\lambda = \sqrt{1-E^{2}}$, from the regular behaviour $G \propto r^{\gamma_\kappa}$, $F/G = (\kappa + \gamma_\kappa)/Z\alpha$, $\gamma_\kappa = \sqrt{\kappa^{2} - (Z\alpha)^{2}}$ (for the finite nucleus, from the regular power series at the origin). The inward solution starts at $r_m + 50/\lambda$ from the decaying asymptote $F/G = -\lambda/(1+E)$. The two are matched at $r_m = (n_r + \gamma_\kappa + 1)/\lambda$ by requiring $G_{\text{out}}F_{\text{in}} - G_{\text{in}}F_{\text{out}}$ to vanish, which avoids the poles of the logarithmic derivative at the nodes of excited states, and the eigenvalue is refined by Brent's method to $10^{-15}$. The integrals $\int G^{2}$ and $\int F^{2}$ are accumulated inside the integrator, so that $\expval{\beta}$ and the admixture come from the same integration that fixes the eigenvalue. Integration uses an eighth-order Runge--Kutta method with relative tolerance $10^{-11}$.
