[#68 Conceptual] 

Hamilton's analogy places classical mechanics beside geometrical optics and wave mechanics beside wave optics. The 0-Sphere model has so far stood on the ray side: internal energy transport follows thermal geodesics of an optical metric, and the internal phase accumulated between the two kernels is a Hamilton principal function. This paper takes the step to waves. The eikonal expansion separates a phase, governed by a Hamilton–Jacobi equation and a phase index, from an intensity transported at the group velocity and governed by a group index; factoring out the rest-energy frequency leaves an envelope that obeys the Schrödinger equation, a heat equation with imaginary diffusivity. The electron then has two tiers: an external envelope that spreads, and an internal transfer whose discrete spectrum is fixed by the energy identity. What the closure of the internal cycle requires of the internal carrier is stated together with the assumptions it rests on.

— Core Equations —

Phase and intensity at the two lowest orders in \(\hbar\) (Sec. III):

\[ \psi=A\,e^{iS/\hbar}:\qquad \partial_tS+H(q,\partial_qS)=0,\qquad \partial_tA^2+\nabla\!\cdot\!\Big(A^2\,\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}\Big)=0 \]

Phase and group indices of a wave at the internal speed (Sec. IV):

\[ n_{\mathrm g}=\frac{1}{\beta_{\mathrm{ZB}}}=24.71\ \ (\text{any origin of energy}),\qquad n_{\mathrm{ph}}=\beta_{\mathrm{ZB}}\ \ (\text{rest energy kept}),\qquad n_{\mathrm{ph}}=\frac{2}{\beta_{\mathrm{ZB}}}\ \ (\text{removed}) \]

Closure of the internal cycle and the action (Sec. IV):

\[ \frac{n_{\mathrm{ph}}}{n_{\mathrm g}}=N,\qquad N=1:\ \ \Delta\mathcal S_{\mathrm{dyn}}=\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{ZB}}}{\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}}=2\pi,\ \ J=h\ \ (\text{count }\theta),\qquad J=\frac h2\ \ (\text{count }\theta/2) \]

The Schrödinger equation as the envelope of the rest-energy carrier (Sec. V):

\[ \Psi=e^{-2\pi i\nu_Ct}\,\varphi,\qquad i\hbar\,\partial_t\varphi=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_e}\nabla^2\varphi,\qquad \frac{\hbar}{2m_e}=\frac{\lambda_C}{2}\cdot\frac{c}{2\pi}=5.79\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{m^2/s} \]

Fourier content of the quartic energy, and of a product with unequal rates (Sec. VII):

\[ \cos^4\frac{\theta}{2}=\frac38+\frac12\cos\theta+\frac18\cos2\theta,\qquad \cos^2\frac{\theta_A}{2}\cos^2\frac{\theta_B}{2}=\frac14+\frac14\cos\theta_A+\frac14\cos\theta_B+\frac18\cos(\theta_A+\theta_B)+\frac18\cos(\theta_A-\theta_B) \]

— Key Contributions —

  • From rays to waves: The two rows of the geodesic hierarchy of Paper #51, the phase functional and the thermal geodesic, are the two lowest orders of the eikonal expansion: phase at order \(\hbar^0\), intensity carried at the group velocity at order \(\hbar^1\). Where the series had one index, there are two.
  • Which index is invariant: The group index \(1/\beta_{\mathrm{ZB}}\) does not depend on the origin of energy; the phase index of a matter wave does. Snell's law and the critical angle of Paper #66, which rest on the phase index, therefore require a statement of the origin from which the internal phase is counted.
  • What the closure fixes: A round trip of length \(\lambda_C\) fixes the ratio \(n_{\mathrm{ph}}/n_{\mathrm g}=N\) of the carrier, not its dispersion. A body of the electron's mass making the round trip at \(v_{\mathrm{ZB}}\) holds \(0.0405\) of its de Broglie wavelength and does not close. For the non-dispersive lowest mode, counting the phase with \(\theta\) gives \(J=h\); counting it with the half angle \(\theta/2\) of the amplitude, which carries the double cover of the spinor, gives \(J=h/2\). The choice between the two counts is left open.
  • The Schrödinger equation as an envelope: Factoring out the rest-energy oscillation \(\nu_C=m_ec^2/h\), which de Broglie attached to the electron, leaves the Schrödinger equation, in the same way that factoring out an optical carrier leaves the paraxial wave equation. It is a heat equation with an imaginary diffusivity, which relates the thermal language of the series to the phase language of wave mechanics.
  • Two tiers: The external envelope has a continuous spectrum and spreads; the internal transfer has a discrete spectrum fixed by the energy identity and restored at every cycle. A free Schrödinger packet of Compton size doubles its width within \(0.44\) of one internal cycle, so the internal tier lies outside the free envelope equation. The two tiers correspond to the convective and spin terms of the Gordon decomposition of the Dirac current.
  • Discrete group velocity and the correspondence principle: The difference quotient of Paper #66 is a further instance of Bohr's replacement of a derivative by a difference over quantum numbers. Because the internal phase advances uniformly, the second line lies at exactly \(2\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}\); written as a product density, unequal rates would split the first line and add sum and beat lines.

— Constraints —

  • The charge dipole may not oscillate: An oscillating charge dipole at \(\nu_{\mathrm{ZB}}\) with amplitude \(\lambda_C/4\) would radiate \(1.0\) W and exhaust the rest energy in \(8\times10^{-14}\) s; the internal transfer must leave the centre of charge fixed.
  • Scattering: The free electron gives the Thomson and Klein–Nishina cross sections through its centre of mass; the internal tier can contribute at most a resonant feature near \(20.7\) keV in the rest frame, which existing Compton-scattering data bound.
